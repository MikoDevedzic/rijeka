\section*{Version history}
{\small
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=4pt]
\item \textbf{1.47 (23 November 2026).} The last step of an option's exposure run on a currency pair or a digital asset (\S\ref{sec:exposure}); the exposure of every such option changes, most where the option is short. An option expires some days before it settles and the run ends on the settlement date: the payoff was fixed on the level at that date, which had moved for those days as well, so the expected value at the last date stood above the option's price by about $\sqrt{T_s/T_e}-1$. The level at the expiry is now drawn inside the last step, the payoff and a barrier's watch read it, and the value a margin period earlier in that step runs to the expiry. An uncollateralised one-week EURUSD option's CVA falls 3 to 6\%, by the two or four days between its expiry and its settlement, a one-month one's 0.3 to 0.5\%, a three-month one's under 0.1\%. In a package every leg, and the value a margin period earlier, reads the one path those levels are on.
\item \textbf{1.46 (23 November 2026).} The XVA workbooks of the currency side are a ticket's own run. The forward books (\code{xva\_fx}, \code{xva\_crypto}) take the forward the ticket's route reads from the account's market, run eighteen months, where a ticket's own grid is monthly, and step each month at the surface's own vol. A European option on a currency pair and on a digital asset has an XVA workbook (\code{xva\_fx\_option}, \code{xva\_crypto\_option}), in the ticket's flat-vol mode. \S\ref{sec:exposure} has a replication paragraph for each; \S\ref{sec:governance} says which mode and which styles are not rebuilt. Two figures change, both found by the option's book: an option's exposure run reads the forward and the discounting at the settlement date, as its price does, and so starts from the ticket's price (it started 0.04 to 0.09\% below it on an eighteen-month EURUSD call and 0.09 to 0.18\% on a three-month one, \S\ref{sec:exposure}); and a pair with no surface saved is run at one default level wherever it is used, a digital asset's option no longer at the currencies' 10\% (\S\ref{sec:fxo}).
\item \textbf{1.45 (23 November 2026).} More of what a ticket shows can be rebuilt in a spreadsheet; no figure of a ticket changes. A floor and a collar have workbooks of their own, for the price (\S\ref{sec:caps}) and for the XVA run (\S\ref{sec:exposure}); a swaption has an XVA workbook (\S\ref{sec:exposure}); the forward workbook of a currency pair is carried from the pair's spot date, as the ticket is (\S\ref{sec:fx}). \S\ref{sec:governance} has a table of which instrument has which workbook, and which has none.
\item \textbf{1.44 (19 November 2026).} The signed record of an option on a rate states the option (\S\ref{sec:chain}, record schema v3). No price, risk or XVA figure of a ticket changes. A premium is booked only where the desk enters one (\S\ref{sec:caps}). The cap's XVA workbook was opened in Excel by the project's owner: every check passed (\S\ref{sec:exposure}). Until this version a swaption's record was the swap it delivers: no buyer or seller, no expiry, no premium; a bought and a sold swaption were the same bytes, and the sold one named the wrong payer of each leg. A cap's, a floor's and a collar's record held no premium. The counterparty's own booking, made from a countersigned record, now holds the opposite side of the option.
\item \textbf{1.43 (19 November 2026).} A cap, a floor and a collar have XVA (\S\ref{sec:exposure}, \S\ref{sec:ccr}). Until this version a ticket's run refused the three and a netting set's left them out. Each period of the option is valued on every path by Black's formula on what one unit grows to over the period, at the variance the paths' own model gives that growth: on the risk-neutral paths exact but for the payment lag, a martingale when discounted along them, and with no step when a period fixes. The same formula now values a cap or a floor on a swap's leg, which was valued as a caplet on a rate fixed at its period's start at the market's volatility: every such leg's exposure changes. What a period pays is in the cash a margined run sets against the margin period's move. On the stack's market, valued 19 November 2026, uncollateralised at 5{,}000 paths, seeds 42, 7 and 11, a bought five-year $4.5\,\%$ cap on ten million: CVA $-2{,}157$ to $-2{,}129$, FCA about $-1{,}396$, no DVA or FBA (a bought cap is never owed by us), KVA $-10{,}917$. The model's variance is not the ticket's volatility: through the ticket's formula it is $71.0$bp where the ticket prices at $81.5$bp, so the paths start about a fifth below the ticket's price for this cap; both are printed on the tab. A workbook, \code{xva\_cap}, rebuilds the run in cells. The table of comparisons in \S\ref{sec:quantlib} is printed again from the suite: 84 lines where it had 70, the comparisons added since it was last printed among them.
\item \textbf{1.42 (19 November 2026).} A cap, a floor and a collar are priced as they are booked (\S\ref{sec:caps}); the value of every such ticket changes, and so does the value of a booked one. The ticket priced a schedule of its own (from two calendar days after the valuation date, weekend or not, ACT/360 whatever its day count, the dates typed on it unread), and a booked option fell to the desk's default volatility of 85bp whenever its remaining life was not a quoted maturity of the surface, which is on all but a few days of its life. The ticket now prices the leg it books, and the surface is read between its maturities. On the market the tests run on (the local stack's seed of 2 November 2026), valued 19 November 2026, a five-year $4.5\,\%$ cap on ten million read $97{,}183$ on its ticket and $105{,}015$ once booked; both are now $97{,}322$. A booked cap, floor or collar, and a cap or a floor on a swap's leg, steps in value on the day of the change; a cap's volatility has no step in the P\&L explain (\S\ref{sec:pnl}), so that day's step is in its residual. A cap's or a floor's strike left blank is at the money; the side signs every figure, a swaption's too (\S\ref{sec:swpn-vol}). The three routes take 1, 2, 4 or 12 periods a year, the frequencies a leg is scheduled at. The cap workbook is the ticket's own trade.
\item \textbf{1.41 (18 November 2026).} The replication of a rates ticket's XVA (\S\ref{sec:exposure}); no figure changes. The workbook rebuilt a generic swap whose legs settle every month on today's discounting, the run of before version 1.34; it now rebuilds the ticket's own run, cell by cell.
\item \textbf{1.40 (18 November 2026).} The volatility of a swaption on a ticket (\S\ref{sec:swpn-vol}); the value of every swaption ticket on which no volatility is typed changes. An untouched ticket priced at $86.5$bp whatever its expiry, its tenor or the day's surface; it now reads the desk's at-the-money swaption grid at the nearest point, the rule a booked option was already revalued on, and says which point and which day. At the money the price is proportional to the volatility, so the change is the ratio of the two: on the reference market of the tests, $94.9$bp at one year into five, $+9.7\,\%$. A currency with no surface takes the desk default of $85$bp, $-1.7\,\%$. A typed volatility is unchanged.
\item \textbf{1.39 (18 November 2026).} The expiry of a swaption on a ticket (\S\ref{sec:swpn-expiry}); a European swaption's ticket value changes where its tenor is not a whole number of 365-day years, and the swap it delivers ends on the right day. A tenor (now one month to ten years) is that many calendar months from the valuation date, or the expiry is a date typed in. The option's time is the days to that date over 365, the convention the booked option was already revalued on; a ticket priced a tenor at its nominal fraction of a year, so a three-month option struck on 18 November 2026 ran $0.25$ where the calendar gives $92/365$, a price $0.41\,\%$ lower at the money. The swap delivered ended a day short whenever a leap day fell inside it. Nothing changes for a booked trade's dates.
\item \textbf{1.38 (18 November 2026).} The hosted demo's interest-rate curves are synthetic (\S\ref{sec:mr}); every rates figure of the demo changes with them. Until now each curve was built from a published overnight fixing and government yields with a stated spread. The firm holds no licence for any benchmark, and some need one to build a curve from or to value with, so the demo no longer reads any: one stated generator makes every curve, every past day's curve and every overnight fixing. A self-hosted desk connects its own market data and none of this applies to it.
\item \textbf{1.37 (17 November 2026).} Wording only; no figure changes. The licensed margin methodology the engine does not compute is named once, in \S\ref{sec:exposure}, where the initial margin is described; elsewhere the margin is called what it is, the desk's amount or the engine's own estimate.
\item \textbf{1.36 (17 November 2026).} Two corrections found by putting one trade through every XVA run (\S\ref{sec:xva}). The counterparty run's capital charge is on each trade's own asset class: it took the interest-rate add-on on the set's gross notional whatever the set held, so the incremental KVA of a currency forward was a seventeenth of its ticket's and a digital-asset forward's was zero. And under a margin agreement a payment takes its collateral with it: a package leg that settled was taken out of the value and not out of the collateral, which left the whole payment owed for half a step (on a margined calendar spread whose near leg pays 1.7 million, a DVA of 49 where it is 0.9).
\item \textbf{1.35 (17 November 2026).} The exposure is discounted on its own path (\S\ref{sec:xva}); CVA, DVA, FCA and FBA of every rates ticket and of every counterparty run change, by about $2\,\%$ on a five-year swap. Each charge is, by definition, on $\E[D(t)\,V^{\pm}(t)]$; the engine had taken $P(0,t)\,\E[V^{\pm}(t)]$, which leaves out that a swap's exposure and its discount factor move together. Found by running one swap through ORE (the Open Source Risk Engine) and this engine like for like, and confirmed against a strip of QuantLib swaptions: the first outside figures for an adjustment.
\item \textbf{1.34 (17 November 2026).} A rates ticket's XVA is of the trade itself (\S\ref{sec:exposure}); every rates ticket's exposure and adjustments change. Until now the ticket ran a generic swap of its notional, coupon and original tenor whose two legs settle every month: a yearly swap's last year of exposure, a stored trade's remaining life and a swaption (whose terms the run did not read: its figures were a swap's) were not in it. The paths now value the trade's own legs on their own dates, by the valuation of \S\ref{sec:ccr}, and a ticket and a netting set of that trade alone are one calculation (within $0.2\,\%$, tested). A cap, floor or collar has no XVA until it is valued on the paths, and the tab says so.
\item \textbf{1.33 (17 November 2026).} No formula changed. The validation paragraph and the cap's time convention are worded by their evidence: the QuantLib reference suite of \S\ref{sec:quantlib}, which runs on every commit, and the market's convention.
\item \textbf{1.32 (17 November 2026).} A correction in counterparty credit (\S\ref{sec:ccr}); every swap's exposure there changes, and with it the report's PFE, EPE and CVA, the incremental charge and the XVA P\&L that reads them. Between coupon dates a swap's floating leg was valued as if it reset on the valuation date, leaving out the interest accrued since its period began: a swap whose two legs pay on the same dates showed the fixed coupon building up with nothing against it, up to a whole coupon before each payment. Each floating period is now valued on its own dates and carries its accrued interest, a payment some days after the period's end included. Found by putting one trade through every XVA run; the ticket's stand-alone XVA figures were not affected, the incremental charge beneath them was.
\item \textbf{1.31 (17 November 2026).} First to default (\S\ref{sec:xva}); CVA and DVA of every run change. CVA counts the counterparty's default only while the desk has not defaulted before it, and DVA the desk's own only while the counterparty has not: each step's default probability is weighted by the other party's survival at the middle of the step, and on a path where a party's default is tied to the asset, by its survival on that path. On the pinned five-year swap with no margin CVA is $2.5\,\%$ smaller and DVA $3.6\,\%$. DVA and FBA now carry one weight and together credit the funding spread once (they stood $4.8$ to $4.9\,\%$ above it). The two figures without the weight are in every answer.
\item \textbf{1.30 (17 November 2026).} The funding adjustment (\S\ref{sec:xva}); FCA, FBA and the totals that carry them change. The benefit is charged on the funding basis, the funding spread less the desk's own credit spread, where it was a fixed $55\,\%$ of the funding spread: DVA already credits the desk with its own credit spread on what it owes, and a benefit at the whole funding spread counted that part twice. Cost and benefit run only while both parties are alive: each step is weighted by the chance that neither has defaulted. A spread curve quoted by tenor is read as a term spread in the funding adjustment as it is in the credit triangle, so each step is charged the forward spread: FCA and MVA on a rising funding curve are higher than before, and a flat spread is unchanged. On the pinned five-year swap with no margin FCA moves from $-1{,}423$ to $-1{,}319$ and FBA from $1{,}532$ to $623$. No adjustment's sign is set at the end of the run: each is reported as it was accumulated. A swap in a currency other than the dollar is simulated on volatility calibrated to dollar swaptions, and its XVA tab now says so (\S\ref{sec:exposure}).
\item \textbf{1.29 (16 November 2026).} A single currency forward and a European option under a scenario are priced by the engine, as every other style already was (\S\ref{sec:fxo}): the forward by the pricer's own line, the European on the forward and the discount factor themselves, so the unshocked figure is the ticket's own to the last digit; the browser's closed form stands in only while the engine's answer is on its way. A package's figures are still the closed form over its legs.
\item \textbf{1.28 (16 November 2026).} One name for a digital asset's basis sensitivity wherever it is read (\S\ref{sec:fx}, \S\ref{sec:fxo}): the Excel replications of the digital-asset forward and option carry \code{CRYBASIS01} with the screens' sign (the negative of the asset-rate bump), where they had kept the earlier name \code{FUND01} and its sign. No figure on any screen changes; the workbook's row changes its name and its sign.
\item \textbf{1.27 (16 November 2026).} A collar's legs are priced on the ticket's own payment periods (\S\ref{sec:caps}): its pricing route had priced both legs quarterly whatever the ticket's frequency. A quarterly collar is unchanged; a collar at another frequency is now the cap less the floor at that frequency. Valued 2 November 2026 on a flat curve (a continuously compounded zero rate of 3.5\%) at a normal volatility of 85bp, a five-year 4.5\%/2.5\% collar on ten million is $-5{,}943$ quarterly, $-3{,}161$ semi-annual, $+2{,}365$ annual and $-7{,}799$ monthly; before, the ticket read $-5{,}943$ at every frequency.
\item \textbf{1.26 (16 November 2026).} The scenario of an option of any style (\S\ref{sec:fxo}): an American, digital, barrier, double-barrier or touch option on a currency pair or a digital asset is priced by the engine at the scenario's spot, forward, discount factor and volatility, and a spot at or beyond a barrier is valued as touched, the limit of the pricer there. A package's option legs read a shock of the surface's tenors each at its own expiry. A Bermudan swaption's volatility shock moves the Hull--White $\sigma$ the tree is built on (\S\ref{sec:caps}, Scenario). No price of a ticket changes.
\item \textbf{1.25 (15 November 2026).} The scenario tab of a rates ticket (\S\ref{sec:caps}): the engine on a moved market, a curve shocked by its quotes and a volatility by its points, the trade held where it was on the smile while the curve moves; no price changes. A currency or digital-asset option's volatility shocked tenor by tenor (\S\ref{sec:fxo}).
\item \textbf{1.23 (15 November 2026).} Names and signs of the valuation adjustments (\S\ref{sec:xva}); no figure changes. The funding cost is FCA and the funding benefit FBA; FVA is the two together. Until this version the cost alone was called FVA. Every adjustment is written signed as its effect on our value, a cost negative and a benefit positive, which is how the engine has always returned them; the formulas had been written as magnitudes. The adjustments are valuation and are filed as such, apart from counterparty risk, which is exposure against limits.
\item \textbf{1.22 (15 November 2026).} The ladder at each quoted tenor (\S\ref{sec:mr}): beside the four buckets, every quote of a curve is moved on its own and the trade repriced, which is the risk at the instrument one would hedge with. A bucket's figure is unchanged; a point is the slope at its quote, so a trade's points add up to its bucket to within the bucket's own convexity. A cap's volatility is read as a continuous function of the strike's distance from the money (\S\ref{sec:caps}): the prices of caps, floors and collars move by a little, and their rate risk no longer depends on the size of the move. Compared with QuantLib at single quotes (\S\ref{sec:quantlib}).
\item \textbf{1.21 (14 November 2026).} A currency non-deliverable forward's fixing (\S\ref{sec:fx}): the rate fixed is the spot rate for delivery on the fixing date's spot date, so an NDF fixed two business days before it settles is worth what the deliverable forward is, in the quote currency ($N_b(X-K)$) or the base currency ($N_b(X-K)/X$), and one that fixes earlier is read at that earlier delivery date and carried to the settlement date in the currency it is paid in; from the fixing date the trade is that one amount, on the fixing a desk enters for the source its record names or, until then, on the stored spot and marked provisional. Until this version an NDF never became a fixed amount, and its record named neither a real fixing date nor the currency it settled in.

\section{Scope and principles}
Rijeka is a front-to-back desk for over-the-counter derivatives: booking, pricing, cashflows, risk, XVA under a credit support annex with initial margin, and bilateral confirmation anchored to Ethereum. Phase~1 covers interest-rate products on overnight-index curves (USD SOFR, EUR €STR, GBP SONIA and others) and non-deliverable digital-asset forwards and options.

Three principles govern the implementation.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{No black boxes.} Each number is traceable to a formula in this document and a function in the repository; the Excel replications reproduce it outside the system.
\item \textbf{One risk taxonomy.} Rate sensitivities are per basis point and spot sensitivities per 1\%. Sensitivities are \code{IR01} (all curves bumped), \code{IR01_DISC} (discount curves only), \code{THETA}, \code{GAMMA}, \code{VEGA}; the terms PV01 and DV01 are not used.
\item \textbf{Market data is dated and sourced.} Every snapshot carries a valuation date and a source. Pricing off a stale snapshot is permitted but is always flagged with the snapshot date; it is never silent.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discount curve construction}\label{sec:curve}
{\raggedright \emph{Module: \code{pricing/bootstrap.py}, \code{pricing/curve.py}.}\par}\nopagebreak

\subsection{Instruments}
A curve is built from an overnight deposit and a strip of OIS par swap rates (tenors in weeks, months and years). Futures, FRAs and IRS quote types are accepted by the engine but Phase~1 curves use deposit and OIS swaps only.

\subsection{Algorithm}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item The overnight deposit gives $P(t_0+1\mathrm{d}) = 1/(1 + r_{\mathrm{ON}}\,\tau_{\mathrm{ACT}/360})$.
\item The spot discount factor $P(t_s)$ is obtained by extrapolating the overnight zero rate to the spot date and is \emph{frozen} as an explicit pillar. This is the step that makes the bootstrap and the pricer agree to the last cent: both read the same $P(t_s)$.
\item Annual OIS swaps are bootstrapped on a dense integer-year grid ($1$Y to the longest quote, each year counted from the spot date, so a 29 February spot rolls to 28 February), with missing years filled by linear interpolation of par rates, so that every annual period end is a pillar. This gap fill is a convention: QuantLib's bootstrap of the same quotes interpolates log-linearly in discount factors between quoted maturities, and the two curves agree at the quotes but part by up to 1.3bp in zero rate (and 4bp in a single three-month forward) between them (\S\ref{sec:quantlib}).
\item Week- and month-tenor swaps are then bootstrapped in maturity order against the pillars already present.
\end{enumerate}
For an OIS swap of par rate $R$ with annual periods $(T_{i-1},T_i]$, $i=1\dots n$, accrual factors $\tau_i$ (ACT/360 for USD), the terminal discount factor solves the par condition with the floating leg replaced by its telescoping value $P(t_s)-P(T_n)$:
\begin{equation}
P(T_n) \;=\; \frac{P(t_s) - R\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\tau_i\,P(T_i)}{1 + R\,\tau_n}.
\label{eq:ois-boot}
\end{equation}
Because every period end is a pillar, a swap struck at its own par rate reprices to exactly zero.

\subsection{Interpolation and extrapolation}
Between pillar dates $d_0<d<d_1$ the discount factor is log-linear in the calendar-day fraction $w=(d-d_0)/(d_1-d_0)$:
\begin{equation}
P(d) = \exp\big[(1-w)\ln P(d_0) + w\ln P(d_1)\big],
\end{equation}
equivalent to a piecewise-constant forward rate. Beyond the last pillar the continuously-compounded zero rate is held flat. Before the first pillar $P=1$. The Excel replications implement exactly this rule with \code{MATCH}/\code{INDEX}, so a reader can check any discount factor the engine produces.

\subsection{Risk bumps}
A parallel shift of $b$ basis points is applied to zero rates: $P_b(T) = P(T)\,e^{-b\cdot 10^{-4}\,T}$ with $T$ in ACT/365F years (\code{Curve.shifted}).

\section{Schedules and cashflows}\label{sec:schedules}
{\raggedright \emph{Module: \code{pricing/schedule.py}, \code{pricing/day_count.py}, \code{pricing/ir_swap.py}.}\par}\nopagebreak

Coupon periods are rolled from an anchor at the payment frequency (monthly, quarterly, semi-annual, annual, or zero-coupon), every date counted from the anchor ($\text{anchor}\pm k$ periods) so that a month-end does not drift: rolled back quarterly from 31 August the dates are 31 May, 28 February, 30 November, 31 August. No end-of-month rule is applied. The stub type chooses the anchor: \code{SHORT_FRONT} (the default) and \code{LONG_FRONT} roll backward from maturity, \code{SHORT_BACK} and \code{LONG_BACK} forward from the effective date, the long variants merging the odd period into its neighbour; explicit first-period-start and last-period-end dates override this.

Period end dates are unadjusted; payment dates apply the business-day convention and the payment lag in business days of the leg's calendar (its \code{payment_calendar}, else its currency's: New York, TARGET, London, Tokyo, Zurich, Sydney, Toronto, or a join of them). Floating fixings are two business days before the period start on the same calendar. A period whose accrual ends on a weekend at month-end is therefore paid on the preceding business day, before its accrual ends; QuantLib, which adjusts accrual dates as ISDA does, would not build such a coupon, and the reference checks reproduce it coupon by coupon.

(Before November 2026 the dates drifted after a short month, the stub type was ignored, and the lag and fixing dates counted weekdays only; NPV moved by less than 0.02bp of notional, individual coupon dates by up to three days.)

A fixed coupon over $(d_{s},d_{e}]$ is $N\,r\,\tau(d_s,d_e)$. A floating coupon on a compounded overnight index is projected from the forecast curve as
\begin{equation}
r_{\mathrm{float}} = \frac{1}{\tau(d_s,d_e)}\left(\frac{P_f(d_s)}{P_f(d_e)} - 1\right) + s,
\label{eq:float}
\end{equation}
which equals the daily-compounded in-arrears rate implied by the curve; $s$ is the spread. For a period already in progress, a realised fixing $R$ (when supplied) covers the elapsed part $\tau_e$ and the curve's forward $F$ the remainder $\tau_r$, compounded: $r=\big[(1+R\tau_e)(1+F\tau_r)-1\big]/\tau$. The realised part is rebuilt from the desk's own market data (\code{pricing/fixings.py}): the overnight rate is the ON quote of every daily snapshot of the leg's curve, carried over days without a snapshot and compounded on the index's basis (ACT/360 for SOFR, \euro STR and SARON, ACT/365F for SONIA and TONA); the price response names the snapshots it used. Without a snapshot on or before the period start the elapsed days are assumed to have accrued at the remaining stub's forward, and the response says so (the error is the realised-minus-forward gap times the elapsed fraction: 11bp of notional in the reference test, where they differ by 40bp). Notional, fixed-rate and spread schedules (step-ups, amortisation) are resolved per period from their effective dates.

Each coupon is discounted at its business-day-adjusted payment date, the date the cash moves. (Engine versions before October 2026 discounted at the unadjusted period end; where a date rolled over a weekend the two differ by a day or two of discounting. Corrected in v0.5, with the Excel replications as the independent check.)

\section{Linear interest-rate products}
{\raggedright \emph{Module: \code{pricing/ir_swap.py}, \code{pricing/greeks.py}.}\par}\nopagebreak

The value of a leg is the signed sum of discounted coupons, $\mathrm{PV} = \pm\sum_k C_k\,P(T_k)$, with pay legs negative. A swap's NPV is the sum of its legs; a basis swap is two floating legs on different forecast curves; an OIS swap is the single-curve case where forecast and discount curves coincide. The par rate is $R^\ast = \mathrm{PV}_{\mathrm{float}}\,/\,(N\sum_i \tau_i P(T_i))$.

Sensitivities are computed by bump-and-reprice:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \code{IR01}: all curves shifted $+1$bp;
\item \code{IR01_DISC}: discount curves shifted $+1$bp with forecasting held fixed (in single-curve mode this requires a separate bumped copy for discounting, so that \code{IR01_DISC} $<$ \code{IR01}; the difference is the forecasting sensitivity);
\item \code{THETA}: $\mathrm{NPV}(t_0+1\mathrm{d}) - \mathrm{NPV}(t_0)$ with curves held;
\item \code{GAMMA}: second difference of $\pm 1$bp shifts.
\end{itemize}

\section{European swaptions and the volatility surface}
{\raggedright \emph{Module: \code{pricing/swaption.py}, \code{pricing/sabr.py}.}\par}\nopagebreak

A swaption under a scenario of its curve or its volatility (the ticket's scenario tab) is described with the caps', in \S\ref{sec:caps} (Scenario).

The figures on a ticket carry its side: every figure of money or of risk on a written swaption is the holder's with the sign turned: the value, vega, the forward delta and the curve's, gamma, theta, volga, vanna, the hedge notional and the model's cross-check value; a ratio or a volatility is not (since version 1.42; until then the ticket and its scenario showed the holder's on either side, so a written option read as an asset and its premium received was added to it; the booked trade's value and its exposure run already carried the side). The option is priced on the swap it delivers between that swap's two dates, the fixed leg taken as paid annually on ACT/360 against the index flat, whatever conventions are written on the legs: the ticket shows those terms as they are priced and booked and does not open them, and offers no cap or floor on the delivered swap's leg, which no swaption pricer reads.

\subsection{Annuity, forward and the Bachelier price}
For an option expiring at $T_e$ into a swap paying annually at $T_1<\dots<T_n$ (dates rolled backward from the swap's maturity, with the trade's spot lag and business-day adjustment when the trade dates are supplied),
\begin{equation}
A = \sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_i\,P(T_i), \qquad F = \frac{P(T_0)-P(T_n)}{A}.
\end{equation}
Rates on overnight curves can be negative and are quoted in normal volatility, so the option is priced in the Bachelier model. With $v=\sigma_N\sqrt{T_e}$ and $d=(F-K)/v$,
\begin{equation}
V_{\mathrm{payer}} = N\,A\,v\,\big[d\,\Phi(d)+\varphi(d)\big], \qquad
V_{\mathrm{receiver}} = N\,A\,v\,\big[-d\,\Phi(-d)+\varphi(d)\big].
\label{eq:bachelier}
\end{equation}
Greeks are produced by bump-and-reprice: vega per 1bp of $\sigma_N$, \code{IR01}, theta per calendar day, volga, vanna, dollar gamma, and a delta-hedge notional. A Hull--White cross-check price is reported alongside when the model is calibrated (\S\ref{sec:hw}).

\subsection{The expiry on a ticket}\label{sec:swpn-expiry}
The expiry is a tenor or a date. A tenor (1M, 3M, 6M, 9M, 1Y, 2Y, 3Y, 4Y, 5Y, 7Y, 10Y) is that many calendar months from the valuation date, on the same day of the month; a date on a Saturday or a Sunday is moved to the Monday after or, where that is in the next month, to the Friday before. The ticket holds no holiday calendar: an expiry that would fall on a holiday is typed as the date wanted. A typed date is taken as typed; one that is not after the valuation date is refused, in words, and nothing is priced or booked on it.

The option's time is $T_e=d/365$ with $d$ the calendar days from the valuation date to the expiry, on a ticket as in the revaluation of the booked option for P\&L, VaR and the ladder (\S\ref{sec:mr}), so a ticket and its booking are one number and a booked option opened later shows the time it has left. Until version 1.39 a ticket priced a tenor at its nominal fraction of a year. At the money the price is proportional to $\sqrt{T_e}$, so the change is $\sqrt{(d/365)/T_{\text{nominal}}}-1$: nothing for a one-year option over 365 days ($+0.14\,\%$ over the 366 of a year with a 29 February in it), $+0.41\,\%$ for three months of 92 days, $+0.21\,\%$ for two years of 733 days (a leap day and a weekend's roll), $+0.04\,\%$ for ten years of 3{,}653 days. That is the time alone, at the money: the forward and the annuity move with the delivered swap's dates by a day or two as well, and away from the money the smile is read at the new time. The exposure paths measure the same option's time in years of 365.25 days (\S\ref{sec:ccr}): $0.07\,\%$ less time, a stated difference between the two engines.

The swap delivered starts as many calendar days after the spot-starting swap of the same tenor as the expiry is after the valuation date, which keeps the trade's spot lag after the expiry, and ends its tenor later by the calendar. Until version 1.39 both dates were moved by one count of days, which ended a five-year swap one day short whenever a 29 February fell inside it. Neither date is adjusted for a weekend or a holiday in the ticket's price (a start on a Saturday is priced from the Saturday; the booked legs carry a business-day convention for their own schedules): a stated limit, not sized. A booked trade keeps its dates: opened again, its ticket reads the expiry from the booked terms and the delivered swap from the booked legs, whichever rule made them; past its expiry it is that swap, and no option is priced.

The ticket's date rule has no reference comparison and no workbook: it is covered by unit tests asserted by hand (\code{product-defaults.test.js}), and the model inventory says so.

A Bermudan (\S\ref{sec:berm}) takes a tenor only and whole years for its time, on a new ticket and on a booked one: its tree steps in twelfths of a year, while its expiry date and the swap's dates are the calendar's, a day or three apart from the tree's at the last exercise.

\subsection{Volatility tiers}\label{sec:swpn-vol}
A swaption is priced at the volatility in its request or, when the request carries none, at the one the desk's market gives: the point of the currency's at-the-money grid nearest the option, nearest in $|\ln(T_e/T_e')|+|\ln(\tau/\tau')|$ over the grid's expiries $T_e'$ and swap tenors $\tau'$ (an expiry under one month counts as one month and a tenor under three months as three). There is no interpolation between points, and beyond the grid's edges the edge stands: a three-month expiry reads the one-year row. The point is read on the latest market date at or before the valuation date, so the same option can read another point, or another level, on another day. A ticket sends no volatility unless one is typed; it shows the number in its volatility field before it is priced, with the point and the surface's date under it.

The same lookup revalues the booked option for P\&L, VaR and the ladder (\S\ref{sec:mr}) and gives the market volatility reported beside an exposure run (\S\ref{sec:ccr}). So, at the money, on the day, an untouched ticket and its booking are valued at one volatility; over the ticket's lists of expiries and tenors the two ask for the same point on every weekday of two years (the validator's sweep; a typed expiry date and the exposure engine's question were not swept). Away from the money they differ where a smile is stored: the ticket's price is then struck at the smile's volatility, also over a typed one, while the booked option is revalued at the at-the-money point. Where no smile is stored a strike away from the money is priced flat at the at-the-money volatility. Both are stated limits.

The volatility is tagged. \code{MANUAL}: the request's own. \code{QUOTED}: read from the grid, the option on a grid point, which is within two per cent of it in the distance above (a year by the calendar is 365 to 368 days, and is the one-year point on every trade date). \code{NEAREST}: read from the grid's nearest point, the option off it. \code{DEFAULT}: the currency has no surface, and the desk default of $85$bp stands in. \code{SABR}, or the name the stored smile's parameters carry: a strike away from the money, priced at the smile's volatility, which the answer reports beside the at-the-money one. The tier travels with the price, with the point and the surface's date, so that a valuation can be challenged; under a scenario's volatility shift they describe the point before the shift. Only the dollar has a surface in the hosted demo, and it is synthetic (\S\ref{sec:mr}); a swaption in another currency is priced at the default unless a volatility is typed, and the ticket says so. The lookup is the desk's own rule: it has no reference comparison and no workbook, only unit tests asserted by hand and the validator's independent sweep. Until version 1.40 a ticket with no volatility typed was priced at $86.5$bp whatever the expiry, the tenor or the day.

\subsection{$\beta=0$ normal SABR}
The smile at each expiry--tenor point is the normal SABR of Hagan et al.\ (2002) with backbone $\beta=0$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\sigma_N(K) &= \alpha\,C(F,K)\left\{1+\left[\frac{2-3\rho^2}{24}\nu^2+\frac{\rho\,\nu\,\alpha}{4}\right]T\right\},\\
C &= \frac{z}{\chi(z)},\qquad z=\frac{\nu}{\alpha}(F-K),\qquad
\chi(z)=\ln\frac{\sqrt{1-2\rho z+z^2}+z-\rho}{1-\rho},
\end{split}
\end{equation}
with $C=1$ at the money. Parameters $(\alpha,\rho,\nu)$ are fitted to quoted volatilities at strike offsets $\{-200,-100,-50,-25,0,+25,+50,+100,+200\}$bp by weighted least squares, with $\alpha$ constrained so the at-the-money volatility is matched exactly. The fit RMSE is stored with the parameters.

\section{Caps, floors and collars}\label{sec:caps}
{\raggedright \emph{Module: \code{pricing/cap_floor.py}, \code{pricing/cap_vol_strip.py}, \code{pricing/cap_sabr.py}.}\par}\nopagebreak

A cap on a compounded overnight index is a strip of caplets on the periods of the floating leg it is booked as (\emph{Booking}, below): from the leg's effective date to its maturity (on a ticket the currency's spot date and the tenor in calendar months, or the dates typed), at its payment frequency, with its day count on the accruals between unadjusted dates, each caplet paid on the adjusted end of its period plus the index's payment lag. The price on a ticket and the value of the booked trade are this one calculation on the same leg (\code{api/routes/pricer.py}, \code{\_cap\_like}; \code{pricing/ir\_swap.py}), and a test holds the ticket's price to the stored trade's value to the cent. Until version 1.42 the ticket priced a schedule of its own: quarterly from two calendar days after the valuation date, which could be a Saturday, on ACT/360 whatever the ticket's day count, discounted at each period's end, and the dates typed on the ticket were not read. At one volatility (85bp, on the reference suite's curve, three five-year options measured before the change) the two were $0.1$ to $0.2\,\%$ of the premium apart on the ticket's own terms and $4$ to $6.5\,\%$ once the day count was changed to 30/360 or ACT/365F; a start date typed three months on changed the booking and not the price. The rate fixes in arrears over the whole period; the caplet is valued with option time to the \emph{middle} of its accrual period, $T=\big[(d_s-t_0)+\tfrac12(d_e-d_s)\big]/365$ (\code{option_time}; the market's convention for a cap on a compounded overnight rate). Time is on ACT/365F because normal vols are quoted per 365-day year; before November 2026 it was measured on ACT/360, which overstated the variance by $365/360$. For caplet $(d_s,d_e]$ with forward $F$ from \eqref{eq:float} and normal volatility $\sigma_N$,
\begin{equation}
\text{caplet} = N\,\tau\,P(d_p)\,\Big[(F-K)\,\Phi(d) + \sigma_N\sqrt{T}\,\varphi(d)\Big],\qquad d=\frac{F-K}{\sigma_N\sqrt{T}},
\end{equation}
with $d_p$ the date the caplet is paid, and a floorlet is the corresponding put. The at-the-money strike of a cap is the annuity-weighted average of its caplet forwards; a cap or a floor whose strike is left blank on a ticket is struck there, to eight decimals, the price says so and the booking carries that rate (a collar has no strike to take from the market: both are typed). A collar is a long cap and a short floor. The figures carry the side: a written option's value, vega and rate risk are the holder's with the sign turned, and a written collar is the floor held and the cap written (until version 1.42 the ticket showed the holder's figures on either side). A cap's flat volatility is read from the surface at the strike's distance from the at-the-money forward, on straight lines through the quoted distances and the at-the-money quote and on the last line beyond them; the distance is signed, and a floor is read at the same point as a cap at its strike. It is a continuous function of the forward. The surface is quoted at a few cap maturities (one, two, three, five, seven and ten years), and an option is read at its remaining life, from the valuation date to the leg's maturity in years of $365.25$ days: within a hundredth of a year of a quoted maturity, there; between two, at the strike's distance from the money on each and on the straight line in maturity between the two; shorter than the first or longer than the last, at that end's. Until version 1.42 a remaining life that was not a quoted maturity fell to the desk's default of 85bp: a five-year cap booked from its spot date is $5.0103$ years from the valuation date, outside that hundredth, inside it for the week after, and outside it from then until its life comes within four days of the next quoted maturity; so a booked cap, and a cap or a floor on a swap's leg, was valued at the default beside a quoted surface on all but a few days of its life (on the stack's market the five-year $4.5\,\%$ cap above at 85bp and $105{,}015$, where the surface gives $81.48$bp and $97{,}322$). The straight line in maturity between flat volatilities is the engine's own choice and has no outside reference. Between two maturities and beyond the ends the reading is labelled interpolated (an end held flat has no label of its own), and the default is named only where a maturity it reads has no quote at all; a maturity with no at-the-money quote takes the default at the money and is labelled by its distances. At the edge of the hundredth the two readings meet to within a two-hundredth of the step between the neighbouring maturities, a few thousandths of a basis point, which is not smoothed. These are the engine's own conventions and have no independent reference (the reference caps and the workbook are given their volatility; since version 1.45 a floor and a collar have workbooks of their own, \code{floor} and \code{collar}, each the ticket as it opens on the same leg, a floorlet the put of the caplet's formula and the collar the cap bought fifty basis points above the at-the-money forward less the floor sold fifty below, every period evaluated outside Excel against the engine's figure for it to a tenth of a cent, and each with a cell for the side that wrote the option; the workbook \code{cap} is the ticket's own trade as it opens, on the leg's periods with each caplet paid two business days after its period, its cells evaluated outside Excel against the engine and its engine figure held to the ticket's route): beyond the last quoted distance the last line carries on, held between 1bp and 500bp; a distance quoted twice counts once, at the mean; one strike has one volatility whichever option is struck there, so that a cap less a floor at one strike is the swap at that strike whatever the volatility; and a surface quoted on one side of the money only is mirrored for a strike on the other side, for a cap as for a floor, where a straight line carried through the money would run on (one quote on a side is enough for that side to be read as quoted, on the line from the money to that quote). The at-the-money forward is that of the option's own schedule, so one strike on two schedules (a cap on its own and a capped leg whose first periods have run off) can be read a fraction of a basis point apart. Until version 1.24 a floor was read at the size of its distance, so a floor struck below the money took the volatility quoted the same distance above it and a cap and a floor at one strike could be priced at two volatilities. Until version 1.22 the distance was rounded to a whole basis point, a strike within five basis points of the money took the at-the-money volatility and one within a basis point of a quote took that quote; each was a step in the price as the curve moved, and a cap's rate risk depended on the size of the move it was measured with. QuantLib's compounded-overnight caplet pricer, which models the backward-looking accrual itself, values the reference caps 0.6--2.0\% of premium lower and the reference floor 2.5\% higher (\S\ref{sec:quantlib}); that is the size of the mid-period convention. Those reference caps are on a quarterly schedule of the suite's own (\code{price\_cap}, which no route calls since version 1.42) and measure the convention, which is the same formula caplet by caplet; the leg's strip a ticket and a booking are valued by is compared with QuantLib's Bachelier formula on the leg's own dates (\code{booked\_cap\_rows}). An index other than the currency's overnight rate (a term rate, the effective federal funds rate) is forecast off the overnight curve: a ticket prices on one curve a currency, the ticket says so beside the index, and a basis between two indices of one currency is not valued.

\emph{Booking.} A cap, floor or collar is booked as one floating leg with zero participation in the rate (\code{leverage} 0) and the strikes on its \code{embedded_options} (a collar: a bought cap and a sold floor, or the reverse), so the swap pricer, the exposure engine and the P\&L value exactly the caplet strip; the side, strikes, vol and premium are also in the trade's terms. A premium is in the terms only where the desk entered one: the model's value is a mid and is not booked in a premium's place (until version 1.44 a cap, a floor or a collar with no premium entered was booked with its value as its premium; a swaption with none). A floating leg's leverage multiplies its projected rate everywhere (booked and ignored before November 2026). Until then these products were booked as a generic two-leg swap with no strike stored.

\emph{Scenario.}\label{sec:scenario} The scenario tab of a rates ticket (a swap, a cap, a floor, a collar, a swaption) is the pricing engine on a moved market; with no shock its request is the request the ticket was priced with and its value the ticket's. A shock of a curve is a shift of the quotes the curve is built from, in basis points: one for every quote and one of its own for a tenor. The tab shows a curve at its main tenors; a quote between two of them takes the shock on the straight line in time between theirs and the end tenor's beyond them (a choice of the tab's, not a market convention), and a quote whose tenor is not a plain tenor takes the flat shock only. The curve is bootstrapped again from the moved quotes and the trade repriced on it in full. The engine takes a shift of at most 1{,}000bp on a quote and the tab one of at most 300bp. A shock of the volatility is a shift, in basis points of normal volatility, of the volatility the trade is priced at: one for the whole surface and one of its own at a maturity of the cap surface or an expiry of the swaption surface (read at the swap tenor stored nearest the trade's). The trade takes the shift at its own maturity or expiry, on the straight line in time between the two points around it and the end point's beyond them; the smile moves in level, not in shape, and a shifted volatility is held at no less than 0.01bp. While the curve moves the trade stays where the saved curve put it: a cap or a floor is read on the surface at its strike's distance from the saved curve's at-the-money forward, a swaption struck at the money keeps the strike the saved curve gave, and one away from the money keeps the volatility the surface gave it there (the moved curve does not read the surface again). The change in value under a curve shock is therefore the price on the curve rebuilt from the shifted quotes, at a fixed volatility; under a volatility shock it is the price at the shifted volatility; and what the two together add beyond their sum is shown as a cross term. Beside them the tab shows the ticket's own IR01 and vega times the shock, which are first-order figures and differ from the repricing by more than curvature: the ticket's IR01 moves the forwards with the discount factors held (a cap's, a swaption's analytic delta) where the scenario moves both, and a quote moved one basis point is not a zero rate moved one basis point. On a flat 3.5\% curve a five-year 4\% cap at 85bp moves by 3.6\% less than its IR01 for one basis point, by 0.5\% more than ten of them for ten, and by a fifth more than fifty of them for fifty. A Bermudan swaption is priced on the tree at the model's own volatility (\S\ref{sec:hw}), the $\sigma$ of the stored calibration, one number with no surface behind it: its volatility shock is a shift of that $\sigma$ in basis points, held at no less than 0.01bp, and the tree is built again; the vega the ticket reports is the central difference of one basis point either side of the same $\sigma$, so a shift of one basis point is the vega to first order, the difference being the tree's curvature in $\sigma$; at the floor no vega is reported, the bump below it being a negative $\sigma$. This is not a shift of the swaption surface: that would be the model calibrated again to the moved surface, which the tab does not do, and a basis point of the short rate's volatility is not a basis point of a swaption's normal volatility. The tab names the factor Hull--White $\sigma$ and offers it flat only. A collar's legs are priced on the ticket's own periods, as a cap's and a floor's are, in a scenario as without one (until 16 November 2026 its route priced them quarterly whatever the ticket said). Since version 1.42 a scenario of a cap, a floor or a collar runs through the leg's strip (the shift of the volatility and the forward the strike's place on the smile is held at are handed to it), which has unit tests of its own and no reference row. The three routes take 1, 2, 4 or 12 periods a year, the frequencies a leg is scheduled at, and refuse any other count (three and six went with version 1.42, when the routes took the leg's schedule). A collar has no outside comparison or workbook of its own: it is the cap less the floor, and a test holds it to them at each frequency. The shifts have no outside reference of their own: the moved market is priced by the functions whose prices are compared with QuantLib (\S\ref{sec:quantlib}). Tests hold that no shift is no change, to the cent; that the saved curve shifted by the engine is the curve of its own quotes shifted and sent; that a volatility shift is the price at the shifted volatility; and, on the test market, that one basis point of curve moves a swap by its IR01 to 2\%, a cap, floor or collar by its IR01 to 10\% and a swaption by its curve delta to 10\% (the tolerance is the discounting and quote-against-zero difference above, so it would not catch an error smaller than that), and one basis point of volatility by the vega to 5\%; and that a Bermudan's price rises with its $\sigma$, one basis point either side being the route's own vega to rounding and one basis point up the vega to 5\%, where the test market holds a calibration (the test is skipped where it does not). That test holds the shift to the $\sigma$ the vega is of; the tree itself is held by its comparison with the Jamshidian closed form (\S\ref{sec:hw}).

Caplet volatilities are stripped from flat cap volatilities one strike column at a time: the 1Y bucket is solved so that four Bachelier caplets reproduce the 1Y cap price; each later bucket (2Y, 3Y, 3--5Y, 5--7Y, 7--10Y) is solved after subtracting the value of the buckets already priced, piecewise flat within a bucket. A $\beta=0$ SABR smile is then fitted per expiry bucket across strikes.

\section{Bermudan swaptions: Hull--White trinomial tree}\label{sec:berm}
\label{sec:hw}
{\raggedright \emph{Module: \code{pricing/bermudan_swaption.py}.}\par}\nopagebreak

The short rate follows $\dd r_t = [\theta(t) - a\,r_t]\dd t + \sigma\dd W_t$. Zero-coupon bonds are affine, $P(t,T)=A(t,T)e^{-B(t,T)r_t}$ with $B(t,T)=(1-e^{-a(T-t)})/a$ (Brigo--Mercurio 2006, eq.~3.39). The tree is the Hull--White (1994) trinomial tree: a symmetric tree in $x_t=r_t-\alpha(t)$ with the standard branching rules, and a deterministic shift $\alpha(t)$ fitted at every grid time by Arrow--Debreu forward induction so that the tree reprices the market discount curve exactly. Backward induction values the underlying swap at each node by the single-curve telescoping identity $\mathrm{PV}_{\mathrm{float}}(t)=\sum_k N_k[P(t,T_{k-1})-P(t,T_k)]$, and applies the early-exercise test at each exercise date.

\textbf{Validation gate.} Before any Bermudan value is trusted, the same tree must price European swaptions within 0.5\% (at the money) and 1\% ($\pm100$bp) of the closed-form Bachelier price on a grid of expiries and tenors. \textbf{Scope.} The model is single-curve and single-tenor: multi-curve underlyings, capped or floored underlyings and basis underlyings are refused rather than approximated, and a Bermudan whose underlying tenor is far from the 5Y calibration column carries a warning.

\section{Hull--White calibration for exposure simulation}
{\raggedright \emph{Module: \code{pricing/calibration.py}.}\par}\nopagebreak

With constant $(a,\sigma)$, the model's at-the-money normal volatility for a swaption with expiry $T_e$ on a swap with payment dates $T_i$, accruals $\alpha_i$ and annuity $A(0)=\sum_i\alpha_iP(0,T_i)$ is (Andersen--Piterbarg 2010, vol.~1 \S4.4)
\begin{equation}
\sigma_N(T_e) = \sigma\,\frac{|w|\sqrt{V(T_e)}}{\sqrt{T_e}},\qquad
w=\sum_i\frac{\alpha_i P(0,T_i)}{A(0)}\,B(T_e,T_i),\qquad
V(T_e)=\frac{1-e^{-2aT_e}}{2a},
\end{equation}
where $P(0,T)$ is the \emph{market} discount factor from the bootstrapped curve (a flat-rate approximation is the fallback only when no curve is supplied). A constant-parameter model cannot fit a whole surface; following standard XVA practice it is calibrated to the 5Y-tenor column ($1$Y$\times5$Y, $2$Y$\times5$Y, $3$Y$\times5$Y, $5$Y$\times5$Y) by least squares, and the fit diagnostics are stored with the parameters. The calibration was verified against Jamshidian's decomposition to within $0.01\%$.

\section{Exposure, collateral and the credit support annex}\label{sec:exposure}
{\raggedright \emph{Module: \code{pricing/csa.py}, \code{pricing/xva_engine.py}, \code{api/routes/xva.py}.}\par}\nopagebreak

\subsection{Simulation}
\textbf{Which volatility.} The Hull--White parameters $a$ and $\sigma$ are calibrated to dollar swaptions, the one swaption surface the system stores. A swap in another currency is discounted and projected on its own curve and simulated on that dollar volatility: there is no market data provider for swaption volatility in other currencies here. Its exposure, and every adjustment read from it, therefore carries dollar rate volatility; the XVA tab of such a swap says so above its figures, and the run's answer names the surface (\code{vol\_basis}).

Short-rate paths are simulated under the calibrated Hull--White model on a monthly grid with the exact fit to the initial curve, and the trade is revalued on every path and date, giving mark-to-market paths $V(t)$ from our side. With $r(t)=f(0,t)+\phi(t)+x(t)$, $x$ the driftless Ornstein--Uhlenbeck deviation and $\phi(t)=\tfrac{\sigma^2}{2a^2}(1-e^{-at})^2$, a bond on a path is
\begin{equation}
P(t,T)=\frac{P(0,T)}{P(0,t)}\exp\!\Big[-B(t,T)\big(x(t)+\phi(t)\big)-\tfrac{\sigma^2}{4a}B(t,T)^2\big(1-e^{-2at}\big)\Big],\qquad B=\frac{1-e^{-a(T-t)}}{a}.
\end{equation}
The counterparty engine (\code{ccr/simulate.py}) omitted $\phi(t)$ until November 2026, which priced bonds high and put path forwards $\phi(t)$ below the curve (8bp at five years, 27bp at ten, for $a=3\%$, $\sigma=85$bp); the risk-neutral martingale check in the reference suite now pins it. Grid times are the grid dates' distance in 365.25-day years, the clock every trade date uses, so a fixing or expiry on a grid date sits on that grid point. A European swaption on the paths is valued before expiry with Bachelier on each path's forward swap rate at the normal vol the paths' own Hull--White model implies for it (the Jamshidian price at $t_0$ inverted through Bachelier, \code{ccr/options.hw_implied_vol}) and exercised at expiry on the same paths, so the value before expiry and the exercise are one model and expected exposure does not step at expiry (with today's market vol instead it stepped by the ratio of the two vols, 13--25\% in the reference test, until November 2026); the market quote is reported beside the vol used. A sold option enters with the opposite sign (the terms' side).

\emph{A cap or a floor on the paths} (since version 1.43; \code{ccr/options.value\_caplets}). A period $(s,e]$ of a cap pays, on its payment date $p$, $N\max(G-X,0)$ with $G$ what one unit placed at $s$ has grown to by $e$ on the path and $X=1+\tau K$; a floor pays $N\max(X-G,0)$. On the paths $G$ is a product of the grid's own bonds (the account over whole steps, and at each end the bond seen on the grid date before), each the exponential of the factor at its grid date, so $\ln G$ is a constant plus $\sum_k w_k\,x(t_k)$ over grid dates: $w_k=B(t_{k+1}-t_k)$ for a whole step, less $B(s-t_k)$ at the step the period starts in, and $B(e-t_k)$ at the grid date before its end, with $B(u)=(1-e^{-au})/a$. Seen from grid date $t_i$ the factor at a later date has variance $\frac{\sigma^2}{2a}\big(1-e^{-2a(t_k-t_i)}\big)$ and the dates after it follow it decayed, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:growthvar}
v_i^2=\operatorname{Var}\big[\ln G\mid x(t_i)\big]=\sum_{k>i}\frac{\sigma^2}{2a}\Big(1-e^{-2a(t_k-t_i)}\Big)\,w_k\,\big(w_k+2S_k\big),\qquad S_k=\sum_{l>k}w_l\,e^{-a(t_l-t_k)} .
\end{equation}
Under the measure of the period's end the mean of $G$ is what the path's bonds give: $M=P(t,s)/P(t,e)$ before the period begins and $M=G(s,t)/P(t,e)$ while it runs, $G(s,t)$ being the growth so far. The caplet is then Black's formula on the growth factor,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:capletpath}
V_{\text{caplet}}(t)=N\,P(t,p)\,\big[M\,\Phi(d_1)-X\,\Phi(d_1-v_i)\big],\qquad d_1=\frac{\ln(M/X)+\tfrac12 v_i^2}{v_i},
\end{equation}
the floorlet the corresponding put, and once the period has ended and waits for its payment date, $N\max(G-X,0)\,P(t,p)$ with the growth the path has realised. A payment after the period's end is discounted to its date as a swap's floating coupon is; the two days' convexity is not in it. On the risk-neutral paths, which are a ticket's run and the pricing run of a netting set, the factor is normal and nothing in this is approximated but the payment lag: the value before a period, during it and at its end are one formula, so the value discounted along the path is a martingale and expected exposure does not step when a period fixes. On the real-world paths of a netting set's risk measures (\S\ref{sec:ccr}), whose increments are drawn from history and whose drift is not the curve's, the growth is not exactly normal and the formula is the value given the path's state under the pricing model, as a swap's is there. The payment lag is approximated in two places: the caplet is discounted from its payment date with the bond to that date in place of the measure of that date, and its payment is set in the grid step that holds the payment date with no interest to the step's end (the convexity of two days is under $0.03\,\%$; the second was not measured). A collar is its cap less its floor on every path, and at one strike a cap less a floor is the swap's floating coupons less the strike's, path by path (both tested). What a period pays is in the cash paid on the path (\code{caplet\_flows}), so a margined run sets it against the margin period's move as it does a coupon. Until version 1.43 a caplet on a swap's leg was Bachelier on the path's forward at the market's at-the-money cap volatility until the period's \emph{start}, and the payoff of that forward after: a rate fixed in advance where the product fixes in arrears, at a volatility that was not the paths' own, and its payment was in no cash flow; a cap, a floor and a collar were not valued at all.

\emph{What the figure is of, and is not.} The variance is the model's, calibrated to swaptions, not the volatility the ticket prices the cap at: the run prints beside the cap the one normal volatility at which the ticket's formula (\S\ref{sec:caps}) would give the model's value today, and the volatility the ticket prices at (on the stack's market $71.0$bp against $81.5$bp for the five-year $4.5\,\%$ cap, whose paths therefore start about a fifth below its price; at the money $70.8$bp against $78.3$bp, about a tenth). The exposure is then of the model's cap, not the market's: with a market volatility above the model's it is understated, by something of the order of that ratio for a cap at the money, whose value is close to proportional to the volatility, and by more for one out of the money; the size was not measured along the profile. A swaption's exposure has the same limit. The paths carry the rate's variance at their monthly dates only: inside a step the account grows at the rate seen at the step's start, so the first step of a period carries no variance of its own. A quarterly period therefore has less variance than the continuous model gives it, and much less when it is near: seen from today, $44\,\%$ less for a period that starts today, $11\,\%$ three months on, $6.5\,\%$ six months on, $3.5\,\%$ a year on, $1.9\,\%$ two years on and $1.3\,\%$ three (the model validator's figures, $a=3\,\%$, $\sigma=85$bp; a ratio, so it does not depend on $\sigma$). The near periods of a cap, which carry little of its value, are the ones most understated. The caplet is valued at the paths' own variance so that it stays a martingale on them, and stands $0.7$ to $3.9\,\%$ below the continuous Hull--White price in the reference comparison (periods one to three years on, \S\ref{sec:quantlib}). A finer grid would close this; it is not done. On a ticket the capital add-on of a cap is on its whole notional, with no adjustment for the option's delta, as a swaption's is there. In a netting set's capital (\S\ref{sec:capital}) a cap and a floor each enter as one option on the rate with the supervisory delta of CRE52.40 and their own side (a cap a call, a floor a put; bought, the cap is long the rate and the floor short it), on the forward of the strip's own periods and exercised at its last period's start: one row an option, not a row a caplet, which is this engine's reading and is stated in the report's notes. A sold cap on a swap's leg, five years at $4.5\,\%$ on ten million on a flat $3.75\,\%$ curve ($a=3\,\%$, $\sigma=80$bp, 20{,}000 paths, seeds 42, 7 and 11), shows a mean exposure against us over its life of $-57{,}900$ to $-58{,}300$ where the valuation before version 1.43, at the 85bp it then read, showed $-62{,}500$ to $-62{,}900$: $7.3$ to $7.4\,\%$ less, and $15\,\%$ less at the peak.

\subsection{Variation margin and the margin period of risk}
Variation margin held at $t$ was called against the exposure one margin period of risk $\delta$ earlier:
\begin{equation}
C(t) = \max\big(V(t-\delta)-\mathrm{TH}_{\mathrm{cp}},0\big) - \max\big(-V(t-\delta)-\mathrm{TH}_{\mathrm{own}},0\big),
\end{equation}
zeroed when $|C|$ is below the minimum transfer amount. The margin period $\delta$ is shorter than a grid step, so $V(t-\delta)$ is sampled from the Brownian bridge between grid points,
\begin{equation}
V(t-\delta)\,\big|\,V(t-\Delta t),V(t)\;\sim\;\N\!\big(V(t-\Delta t)+w\,\Delta V,\;w(1-w)\,s^2\big),\qquad w=\frac{\Delta t-\delta}{\Delta t},
\end{equation}
with $s^2$ the cross-path variance of $\Delta V$. This preserves the variance of the move over the margin period; linear interpolation would understate it by a factor $\delta/\Delta t$.

The bridge above is between \emph{values} and is Gaussian whatever the value is. On a currency or digital-asset ticket (\S\ref{sec:fx}) the value is a function of one lognormal level, every forward of the pair being $F_i(0)\,M_t$, and the bridge is taken where it is exact (since v1.14). Inside a grid step $\ln M$ is a Brownian motion with that step's volatility $\sigma_j$, so
\begin{equation}
\ln M(t-\delta)\,\big|\,\ln M(t-\Delta t),\ln M(t)\;\sim\;\N\!\big(\ln M(t-\Delta t)+w\,\Delta\ln M,\;\sigma_j^2\,w(1-w)\,\Delta t\big),
\end{equation}
and each leg is valued on that level with the time it then has left: a forward as $\pm N\big(F_0\,M(t-\delta)-K\big)\,\mathrm{DF}_q(t-\delta,T)$, an option by its style's closed form, a barrier or a touch carrying what the grid dates before it have seen. A leg that has settled stands at the amount it paid; before today the value is today's. A margin period of a whole number of steps reads the grid itself. An option's expiry lies inside a step, between two grid dates: the level at the expiry is drawn there in the same way, a bridge between the two grid levels with one normal of its own, the payoff is fixed on it, and it is part of the one path every leg reads: in that step the level a margin period earlier is drawn between the levels already drawn either side of it (the step's start and the level at the expiry when the date is before the expiry, the level at the expiry and the step's end when it is after), so a forward and an option of one package are valued on the same level there, and the option is the closed form with the time left to the expiry, or its fixed payoff once the expiry has passed. Several expiries in one step are drawn in order of time, each between the one before it and the step's end; expiries in different steps have draws of their own (version 1.47; until then the payoff was fixed on the grid date after the expiry and the time left ran to that date). A barrier is watched on the spot the forward at the expiry implies, a few days' carry from the forward the payoff is read on. One approximation is the paths' own. A barrier or a touch carries the knock state of the grid dates before $t-\delta$ and is then valued by a closed form that monitors continuously: a path beyond the barrier at $t-\delta$ that has not knocked on the grid is valued as knocked, though about a third of such paths are back inside at the next grid date, which overstates the move near the barrier (by about 13\% of the margin called at one date of a six-month up-barrier at 115\% of spot and 80\% volatility) until the paths monitor between grid dates. The rates ticket and the netting-set engine (\S\ref{sec:ccr}) keep the bridge between values. \emph{Functions \code{exact_lag}, \code{lagged_values}.}

\textbf{Replication of a forward's run} (version 1.46). The workbooks \code{xva\_fx} and \code{xva\_crypto} are the run of a forward ticket's XVA tab: buy EUR 10m, or 10 BTC cash-settled in USD, eighteen months forward at the fair forward. The forward, its discount factor and its spot sensitivity are the ones the ticket's route reads from the account's market (the pair's stored spot, curves and points, the spot date), and at eighteen months the ticket's own grid is the monthly one the book is written on (nineteen steps where the settlement, eighteen months from the spot date, is a little over a year and a half from today). Where the pair has a surface the paths step its at-the-money term structure, as the ticket does by default: the Calibration sheet rebuilds each step's vol from the quotes,
\begin{equation}
\sigma_k^2\,\Delta=\max\!\Big(\sigma_{\mathrm{ATM}}(t_k)^2\,t_k-\sigma_{\mathrm{ATM}}(t_{k-1})^2\,t_{k-1},\ \big(\tfrac14\,\sigma_{\mathrm{ATM}}(t_k)\big)^2\Delta\Big),
\end{equation}
and the Paths sheet steps $F_k=F_{k-1}\exp\!\big(-\tfrac12\sigma_k^2\Delta+\sigma_k\sqrt{\Delta}\,Z_k\big)$; a digital asset's steps run on the clock of its vol regimes (\S\ref{sec:regimes}). The rest is the forward's value on each path, the level bridged in logs a margin period earlier and the trade valued on it, the collateral, the exposures and the waterfall, in cells. Two ties are tested: the cells against the engine under each margin preset on five valuation dates, with a surface (97 to 101 checks, by the number of steps) and without one (79 to 82), and the engine's figures against \code{POST /api/xva/currency} for the same forward, paths and seed, to the cent. Until this version the books priced a six-month forward from illustrative inputs at one vol on monthly steps, where a six-month ticket runs weekly ones: they held the engine's arithmetic, not a ticket's run. \emph{Not in the book:} a ticket's fees; wrong-way risk as a default intensity tied to the asset (\S\ref{sec:xva}; the book has the multiplier alone, at one); the capital of a Group 2b digital asset (the book's asset is Group 2a); the two older capital models, which are in the cells and are not checked; a margin period of a grid step or longer.

\textbf{Replication of an option's run} (version 1.46). The workbooks \code{xva\_fx\_option} and \code{xva\_crypto\_option} are the run of an option ticket's XVA tab in its flat-vol mode, for a bought European call struck at the forward, eighteen months: on EUR 10m against USD, and on 10 BTC cash-settled in USD. One vol, the surface's at the option's expiry and strike (rebuilt on the Calibration sheet, the smile's fixed point iteration by iteration), drives every path and values the option on every path. Before the expiry the option is valued on each path as the ticket prices it, on the forward to settlement $F_t$ and discounted from the settlement date, with the time left to the expiry:
\begin{equation}
V(t)=\pm N\,\mathrm{DF}_q(t,T_s)\,\big[F_t\,\Phi(d_1)-K\,\Phi(d_2)\big],\qquad \tau=T_e-t,\quad d_{1,2}=\frac{\ln(F_t/K)\pm\tfrac12\sigma^2\tau}{\sigma\sqrt{\tau}},
\end{equation}
a put by the same formula with the signs turned; the expiry lies inside the last step, where the forward at the expiry is $F_e=\exp\!\big(\ln F_{n-1}+\omega_e(\ln F_n-\ln F_{n-1})+X\sigma\sqrt{\Delta\,\omega_e(1-\omega_e)}\big)$ with $\omega_e$ the share of the step before the expiry and $X$ a normal of its own, and at the last grid date, the settlement date, the option is its payoff on it, $\pm N\max(F_e-K,0)$. For the other styles, whose closed forms take a spot, a rate and a carry, the same reading is the rate and the carry implied over the time left from $\mathrm{DF}_q(t,T_s)$ and $F_t/S_t$, as the pricer implies them today (\S\ref{sec:fxo}). A margin period earlier it is the same closed form on the level bridged there, with the time left to the expiry; in the last step that level is on the bridge from the step's start to $F_e$, since the payoff is fixed on $F_e$ (the book's margin periods are longer than the days from expiry to settlement; a shorter one lands after the expiry, where the option is its fixed payoff). A digital asset's spot moves on the clock of its vol regimes (\S\ref{sec:regimes}) and the option is still valued at the one vol on every path. The capital add-on is the class's supervisory factor on the notional at spot times the option's supervisory delta (CRE52.40: the Black delta of the strike at the class's supervisory volatility, 15\% for a currency pair and 120\% for a digital asset), held along the grid. A bought option owes nothing on any path, so without collateral DVA and FBA are nil; under a credit support annex the residual takes both signs. Two ties are tested: the cells against the engine under each margin preset on five valuation dates, with a surface and without one (some ninety checks, the option's value on single paths among them), and the engine's figures against \code{POST /api/xva/currency-option} in the flat-vol mode, for the option as the ticket prices and books it, to the cent.

\emph{Not rebuilt.} The ticket's default mode steps the paths on the surface's term structure and reads the smile at each path's moneyness, the paths grouped into eight by their vol and each group valued at its mean vol: that mode has no workbook. Neither have the other styles (American, digital, barrier, double barrier, touch). A sold option is shown by turning the book's Side cell and is not checked. As in the forward books, a ticket's fees, wrong-way risk as a default intensity tied to the asset, the capital of a Group 2b digital asset and a margin period of a grid step or longer are not in the book, and the two older capital models are in its cells unchecked. The book is written for an expiry inside the grid's last step, which an eighteen-month option's is.

\emph{What the book showed, and what was changed for it} (version 1.46, with the app's 0.63.0). Until this version the paths read the forward and the discounting at the expiry: the run's value today was $N\,e^{-r_qT_e}[F_e\Phi(d_1)-K\Phi(d_2)]$ with $F_e=S_0e^{(r_q-r_b)T_e}$, where the ticket prices the option on the forward to settlement, discounted from the settlement date. The two differed by the carry and the discounting over the days between the expiry and the settlement: on the checks' market the run started below the ticket's price by 0.04 to 0.10\% for an eighteen-month EURUSD call and by 0.09 to 0.18\% for a three-month one (two or four days between the two dates), and within 0.01\% of it for the digital asset's (one day), and every exposure carried that: measured before against after, an uncollateralised EURUSD call's CVA and FCA rise by those amounts, a margined one's charges move under 0.1\%, a digital asset's under 0.01\%. The paths now read both at the settlement, on every path and a margin period earlier, for every style; the run's value today is the ticket's price, which the book checks (an American option starts from its tree price and is run as its European on the paths, a lower bound, as before), and the netting-set engine (\S\ref{sec:ccr}) already read it so, with the path mean of the implied rates where this run has one flat rate. The reading assumes what the price assumes: rates that are not random, and a payoff on the forward to settlement at the expiry, so the strike is compared with that forward. For a style that reads the path between the two dates it is the pricer's own approximation: a barrier sees the carry of the days to settlement inside the option's life, and a payment at a hit is discounted at the scaled rate (at most a few hundredths of one per cent of the payout). A grid date within an hour before the expiry is read as the expiry's, the payoff: asked for minutes of time left the closed forms of a touch, a barrier and a corridor lose their arithmetic.

\emph{The last step} (version 1.47, with the app's 0.64.0). Until this version the paths fixed the payoff on the grid date at or after the expiry, which for a single option is the settlement date, and a margin period earlier in that step the time left ran to that date. The level had then diffused for the days between the expiry and the settlement, which the price does not count: the expected value at that one date stood above the ticket's price by about $\sqrt{T_s/T_e}-1$, measured at $+2.0\%$ for a three-month EURUSD call with four days between the two, and by the same rule 13 to 25\% for an option of one week. With the level drawn at the expiry the discounted value is a martingale to the last date, within the error of 200,000 paths at one week and at three months. Measured before against after on tickets of four tenors (the checks' market valued on 3 August and on 12 November 2026, 20,000 paths), the effect follows the days between the expiry and the settlement: an uncollateralised EURUSD call's CVA and FCA fall 3.4\% at one week with two days between and 6.4\% with four, 0.3 to 0.5\% at one month, under 0.1\% at three months and nothing at eighteen; margined, its total falls 3 to 11\% at one week, 1 to 5\% at one month and 0.6 to 2.3\% from three months to eighteen, where the margin at the last date had been read on a move to the wrong date; a BTC option's uncollateralised CVA falls 2.1\% at one week with one day between and 8.2\% with three (a weekend), 0.1 to 0.5\% at one month, its margined total 1 to 4\% at one week and one month and under 0.5\% beyond. On a digital asset the regimes' own bias (\S\ref{sec:regimes}), which the last step had been hiding at short expiries, is what is left there. The netting-set engine (\S\ref{sec:ccr}) still fixes an option's payoff on its own grid date.

\subsection{Volatility regimes of a digital asset}\label{sec:regimes}
A digital asset's daily returns are not draws from one bell curve. Violent days come together and pass within two or three days, and under that the whole level of activity is higher for a couple of months and then lower. One volatility reads the moves over a margin period too small, which is where collateral and initial margin are measured. On a digital-asset ticket (since v1.15) the volatility of day $d$ is
\begin{equation}
\sigma_d \;=\; \sigma\; f_d\; s_d ,
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ is the volatility the ticket carries (the surface's, or the desk's) and $f$ and $s$ are two independent two-state Markov chains: $f$ calm or stressed, $s$ low or high. A chain with high-to-low ratio $\rho$ and daily probabilities $a$ of staying low and $b$ of staying high spends a share $p=(1-a)/(2-a-b)$ of its days high, and its two levels are
\begin{equation}
\ell_{\mathrm{low}}=\big(1-p+p\,\rho^2\big)^{-1/2},\qquad \ell_{\mathrm{high}}=\rho\,\ell_{\mathrm{low}},\qquad\text{so that}\quad (1-p)\,\ell_{\mathrm{low}}^2+p\,\ell_{\mathrm{high}}^2=1 .
\end{equation}
Each path starts in each state with its long-run share; today's regime is not read.

The regimes change the clock and not the law given the clock. With $U_j$ the average of $f_d^2 s_d^2$ over the days of grid step $j$ (the variance the step accumulated on the path, as a multiple of $\sigma_j^2\Delta t$), the level of \S\ref{sec:fx} steps as
\begin{equation}
\ln M(t_j)-\ln M(t_{j-1}) \;=\; -\tfrac12\,\sigma_j^2\,\Delta t\,U_j \;+\; \sigma_j\sqrt{\Delta t\,U_j}\;Z_j ,\qquad \E[U_j]=1 ,
\end{equation}
with the same standard normals $Z_j$ a run on one volatility draws. Three things follow. $M$ is still a martingale, so every forward starts at its own price. The variance expected to any date is $\sigma^2 t$, so today's value of a forward or an option is unchanged and the volatility on the ticket means what it says. An option on the paths is still marked at the surface's volatility, which is not what the regime paths pay on average: an option at the money pays less than its mark when the variance to expiry is uncertain, by about 5.5\% one week from expiry, 3.6\% at one month, 1.2\% at six months and 0.4\% at two years. To that extent an option's simulated value is not a martingale, and its exposure carries a bias of that size. And an uncollateralised ticket's exposure, which is set by the spread of the level at each date, moves little: an exposure at the money is lower when the variance to a date is uncertain than when it is fixed at the same average, by 1 to 3\% on tickets of a year or less. What changes most is the move over days and weeks. The value a margin period earlier is the bridge of \S\ref{sec:exposure} on the clock: the share $\Omega$ of the step's variance that came before $t-\delta$ takes the place of the share $w$ of its time, and the step's variance is the path's own, $\ln M(t-\delta)\mid\cdot\sim\N\big(\ln M(t_{j-1})+\Omega\,\Delta\ln M,\;\sigma_j^2\,\Delta t\,U_j\,\Omega(1-\Omega)\big)$. The bridge draws nothing more than it did.

They are dated constants in \code{pricing/vol_regimes.py}; a refit is a reviewed change. The fast chain is calm or stressed, the slow one low or high; a ratio is the higher volatility over the lower, and the other columns are how long a spell in each state lasts on average. The chains are named by how long their high state lasts. SOL's fast chain is calm for 37 days at a time, close to the spells of its slow chain, so its two chains are less clearly told apart than BTC's or ETH's.

\textbf{The backtest.} Does the simulated level cover what the level did? After the Basel Committee's sound practices for backtesting counterparty credit risk models (December 2010): on every day of the five years (the first 90 are warm-up) the model is given a volatility and asked for the 1st, 5th, 95th and 99th percentile of the log move over the next 5, 10, 14, 30 and 91 days, and the move that followed is counted as beyond each or not. The volatility given is the realised volatility of the 90 days before, since the history of the implied volatilities a ticket carries is not held; the drift is the martingale's. Days overlap and the volatility is itself an estimate, so a binomial band would be wrong: the same procedure is run on 500 histories simulated from the model itself, and the band is where 95\% of those counts fall. What the backtest is not. The volatility in it is realised, not the implied volatility of a ticket. The up side is less comfortable than the down side: BTC's 14-day move went above the 99th percentile on 2.96\% of days, at the very edge of the band, and SOL's on 2.44\%; the model is close to symmetric in logs and these coins' rallies were not. That percentile is the margin the desk calls on a position worth more to it when the coin rises (a forward bought, a call bought): the side on which the margin was exceeded most often, inside the band but not by much. The bands are wide, from about 0.2 to 3\% at the 1st percentile, and their lower edge is at or near zero: the test rejects a model whose tails are too thin; it does not certify a good one, and it cannot reject one whose tails are too wide (BTC's 91-day move never went below the regimes' 1st percentile). The realised volatility of the last 90 days already carries the slow regime of the moment, and the regimes add their slow variation on top of it, so in a high-volatility spell the forecast may lean wide; this was not measured. It tests the level and not a portfolio: an option's exposure also depends on the volatility the paths carry, which is not backtested. The margin period of ten business days is $10/250$ of a year, 14.6 days on the regimes' clock of 365; the test's 14 days are a little short of it.

\textbf{Limits.} Two states a chain is a coarse picture of volatility; the stress the chains produce is milder than the worst the history has. The spells are of days and of months: nothing here makes the level of volatility itself uncertain over years, so the uncertainty in the variance to a date falls with the tenor (its coefficient of variation is about a half at one month, a third at six months and a sixth at two years) and a ten-year ticket's tails are close to a lognormal's again: on long tickets the regimes understate. Default and the regimes are independent: a stressed state does not make default likelier, which understates wrong-way risk when stress and default come together. A barrier or a touch is watched on the grid dates of the regime paths and marked by a closed form that watches continuously at the surface's volatility; the difference that was there before is carried over and has not been measured again on these paths. The closes the fit read are fetched, not kept in the repository: the table is reproduced by running the script, on whatever the source then returns. The parameters are fitted to spot returns and held fixed over the life of the trade, to ten years on the longest tickets. Today's state is not inferred, though the last weeks' returns say something about it: a ticket priced in a stressed month and one priced in a calm month start from the same mix. The volatility on the ticket is read as the average over the trade's life. The regimes drive the level of a ticket's XVA only: the counterparty-credit simulation of \S\ref{sec:ccr} and the market-risk history of \S\ref{sec:mr} are unchanged, the first on one volatility per factor and the second on the history itself. A currency pair keeps one volatility.

\subsection{Initial margin}
Initial margin is one of two things. \textbf{An amount the desk sets}, in the trade's currency, exchanged each way and held for the life of the trade. Or \textbf{the engine's own estimate} from the simulated paths: at each grid date $t_i$, with $\Delta_i = V(t_i)-V(t_i-\delta)$ the change in the set's value over the margin period (the lagged value of the bridge above) and $\bar\Delta_i$ its mean across paths,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{IM}_{\mathrm{recv}}(t_i) = \max\big(q_{0.99}(\Delta_i-\bar\Delta_i),\,0\big),\qquad
\mathrm{IM}_{\mathrm{post}}(t_i) = \max\big(q_{0.99}(\bar\Delta_i-\Delta_i),\,0\big),
\end{equation}
the 99th percentile rise (the margin we call) and fall (the margin we post). The confidence is the one the margin rules for non-centrally cleared derivatives name (BCBS--IOSCO: a one-tailed 99 per cent interval over the margin horizon). The mean is removed because what every path does over the period (a scheduled payment, the passage of time) is not a risk. The estimate is at the model's current volatility, not one calibrated to a period of stress, so it can read lower than a regulatory margin model; it is a cross-section of the paths at each date, not a margin recomputed on each path. On currency and digital-asset tickets the value a margin period earlier is exact (above), and against a simulation that draws the level at both dates, with no grid and no bridge, the estimate agrees to sampling error (about 1\% at 200{,}000 paths; \code{tests/test\_fx\_xva\_lag.py}). Bridging between values, as those tickets did until v1.14 and as the rates ticket and the netting-set engine still do, keeps the variance of the move but is Gaussian where the value is lognormal: within about 1\% at a currency's volatility, and 9 to 18\% low on the margin called at volatilities of 55 to 110\%. Exposure beyond the margin is then the 1\% tail of the model's own move, so a margined CVA is small by construction: gap risk, jumps and a margin call that is not met are not in it. A leg of a package that settles is a payment, not a move, and is not margined. Received IM reduces exposure; posted IM is funded and drives MVA. \emph{Function \code{model_im}.}

\textbf{Rijeka does not compute ISDA SIMM.} SIMM is ISDA's licensed methodology and its risk weights are not in this engine. A firm with a SIMM licence enters its own figure as the amount.

\subsection{Residual exposure}
\begin{equation}
E^+(t) = \max\big(V(t)-C(t)-\mathrm{IM}_{\mathrm{recv}}(t),\,0\big),\qquad
E^-(t) = \min\big(V(t)-C(t)+\mathrm{IM}_{\mathrm{post}}(t),\,0\big).
\end{equation}
Expected exposure $\mathrm{EE}(t)=\E[E^+(t)]$, expected negative exposure $\mathrm{ENE}(t)=\E[E^-(t)]$ and the 97.5\% potential future exposure are reported per date.

\subsection{SA-CCR}
For margined netting sets the maturity factor is $\mathrm{MF}=1.5\sqrt{\max(\delta,\text{floor})/250}$ and replacement cost $\mathrm{RC}=\max(V-C,\;\mathrm{TH}+\mathrm{MTA}-\mathrm{NICA},\;0)$ with $\mathrm{NICA}$ the IM received; unmargined sets use $\mathrm{MF}=\sqrt{\min(M,1)}$ and $\mathrm{RC}=\max(V,0)$. The interest-rate supervisory factor is 0.5\% and $\alpha=1.4$. Capital is 8\% of risk-weighted exposure and feeds KVA.

\subsection{The add-on of a currency or digital-asset ticket}
A currency or digital-asset ticket takes the add-on of its own asset class (\code{pricing/fx_xva.py}, \code{capital_inputs}; \code{pricing/sa_ccr.py}).

\textbf{A currency pair}: the foreign-exchange class, supervisory factor 4\% on the notional of the foreign leg in the domestic currency at spot, one hedging set per pair (CRE52.35, CRE52.72).

\textbf{A Group~2a cryptoasset}: SA-CCR with a cryptoasset class in the structure of the foreign-exchange class, a hedging set per pair, supervisory factor 32\% and supervisory option volatility 120\% (SCO60.98).

\textbf{A Group~2b cryptoasset}: exposure $=\alpha\,(\mathrm{RC}+\mathrm{PFE})$ with the $\mathrm{PFE}$ 50\% of the gross notional of each transaction, no hedging set and no multiplier (SCO60.99).

A forward enters at $\delta=\pm1$ and an option at the supervisory delta of its strike (CRE52.40); the legs of one ticket net inside the hedging set, except under Group~2b.

A cryptoasset is Group~2b unless the bank has shown its supervisor the hedging recognition criteria of SCO60.55 (a regulated derivative or exchange-traded product, USD~10bn of average market capitalisation, USD~50m of trimmed mean daily volume, a year of price observations); the engine's default is Group~2a for BTC and ETH and Group~2b otherwise, and the desk sets the group on the ticket. A style whose size is a payout enters at its payout: the rules name no notional for it, and that is this engine's reading.

\section{The XVA waterfall}\label{sec:xva}
{\raggedright \emph{Module: \code{pricing/xva_engine.py}, function \code{run_waterfall}.}\par}\nopagebreak

Default probabilities come from the credit triangle: hazard rate $h = s/\mathrm{LGD}$ for a flat or term-structured spread $s$, survival $Q(t)=e^{-ht}$. With $D_i=P(t_i)$, $\Delta t$ the grid step and $\Delta Q_i = Q(t_{i-1})-Q(t_i)$,
\begin{align}
\mathrm{CVA} &= -\mathrm{LGD}_{\mathrm{cp}}\sum_i \mathrm{EE}_i\,\Delta Q^{\mathrm{cp}}_i\,\bar Q^{\mathrm{own}}_i\,D_i\;\omega_i, &
\mathrm{DVA} &= \mathrm{LGD}_{\mathrm{own}}\sum_i |\mathrm{ENE}_i|\,\Delta Q^{\mathrm{own}}_i\,\bar Q^{\mathrm{cp}}_i\,D_i,\\
\mathrm{FCA} &= -\sum_i s^{\mathrm{ftp}}_i\,\mathrm{EE}_i\,S_i\,\Delta t\,D_i, &
\mathrm{FBA} &= \sum_i \max\!\big(s^{\mathrm{ftp}}_i-s^{\mathrm{own}}_i,\,0\big)\,|\mathrm{ENE}_i|\,S_i\,\Delta t\,D_i,\\
\mathrm{KVA} &= -\sum_i \kappa\,K_i\,\Delta t\,D_i, &
\mathrm{MVA} &= -\sum_i \mathrm{IM}_i\,\max(s^{\mathrm{ftp}}_i-\pi,0)\,\Delta t\,D_i,
\end{align}
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ColVA} = \sum_i \big(\mathrm{VM}^{\mathrm{post}}_i\,s^{\mathrm{post}} + \mathrm{VM}^{\mathrm{recv}}_i\,s^{\mathrm{recv}}\big)\,\Delta t\,D_i,
\end{equation}
with $\bar Q_i=\tfrac12\big(Q(t_{i-1})+Q(t_i)\big)$ a party's survival at the middle of step $i$. Each adjustment is signed as its effect on our value: a cost is negative and a benefit positive, and the total is their sum. FCA is the cost of funding what is owed to us and is not collateralised, FBA the benefit of funding where we owe, and the funding valuation adjustment is the two together, $\mathrm{FVA}=\mathrm{FCA}+\mathrm{FBA}$; it is two of the components and is not added to the total a second time. (Until version 1.23 the cost alone was called FVA, here and on the screens, and these formulas were written as magnitudes.) Here $\omega_i$ is an optional wrong-way-risk multiplier, $\kappa$ the capital hurdle rate, $K_i$ the SA-CCR capital on the exposure path with the trade's remaining life read at the middle of step $i$ (capital and margin are held through the step, so the step that ends on the maturity is charged like every other; until 0.27.0 the life was read at the step's end, where it is zero, and a one-month trade on its two monthly steps was charged for one), and $\pi$ a yield pick-up on posted collateral (tokenised collateral that earns a return reduces MVA). ColVA is the carry of the variation margin against cash at the OIS the discounting assumes: $\mathrm{VM}^{\mathrm{post}}_i$ and $\mathrm{VM}^{\mathrm{recv}}_i$ are the mean margin posted and held at step $i$ (the two sides of $C$ in \S\ref{sec:exposure}), $s^{\mathrm{post}}$ and $s^{\mathrm{recv}}$ the carries over OIS of what each side is held in (\S\ref{sec:capital}); cash on both sides gives zero, so every figure that predates instruments is unchanged. The waterfall is reported gross and under the CSA, so the benefit of collateral is visible term by term.

\textbf{The exposure is discounted on its own path} (since version 1.35). Each charge above is an expectation of a discounted amount: in CVA, $\mathrm{EE}_i\,D_i$ stands for $\E\big[D(t_i)\,V^+(t_i)\big]$, what the desk is owed at $t_i$ discounted on the path it is owed on, and $|\mathrm{ENE}_i|\,D_i$ for $\E\big[D(t_i)\,V^-(t_i)\big]$; FCA and FBA read the same two, and ColVA the margin balances the same way. Where rates are simulated the engine computes $\E[D(t_i)V^+(t_i)]=P(0,t_i)\,\E[w_i\,V^+(t_i)]$ with $w_i=D(t_i)/P(0,t_i)$ the path's own discounting over today's curve's, $D(t_i)=1/A(t_i)$ on the account of \S\ref{sec:ccr}; the expectation of $w_i$ is one at every date (the sample's mean is within its sampling error of one, tested), and the engine sets the sample mean to one exactly, so that the sampling error of the paths' own mean discount factor is not carried into a charge and an exposure that does not move with rates is charged as before. Until version 1.35 it took the product of the means, $P(0,t_i)\,\E[V^+(t_i)]$, which is the same thing only where discounting and exposure are independent. They are not on a rates trade: a receive-fixed swap is owed most on the paths where rates have fallen, which are the paths discounted least, so the product of the means understates what it is owed and overstates what it owes, and a pay-fixed swap the other way; the difference grows with the life of the trade and with volatility. \emph{Two outside figures.} (i) With the exposure read once a year, on the coupon dates, a swap's unilateral CVA is $\mathrm{LGD}\sum_k \mathrm{Swaption}^{\mathrm{rec}}_k\,\Delta Q_k$ and its DVA the payer swaptions': on a six-year receive-fixed swap at par, under the same Hull--White parameters, curve and survival curves, the waterfall stood over four seeds of 40{,}000 paths between $-0.41$ and $+0.04\,\%$ of QuantLib's Jamshidian strip on CVA and between $-0.70$ and $+0.47\,\%$ on DVA (one seed carries about half a per cent of sampling error); with the product of the means it stood between $-2.16$ and $-2.58\,\%$ and between $+1.58$ and $+2.80\,\%$, and on the same paths, where that error cancels, $2.2\,\%$ lower on CVA and $2.3\,\%$ higher on DVA at every seed (two rows of the reference suite, \S\ref{sec:quantlib}, and a test of that difference; the model validator's own paths, a five-year swap on a flat curve at 400{,}000 paths: $1.8\,\%$ each way). (ii) ORE, the Open Source Risk Engine (version 1.8.17, an exposure and XVA engine built on QuantLib), on a five-year receive-fixed overnight swap of 10 million with the same curve, dates, Hull--White parameters and survival curves, on a weekly grid: expected exposure within $1\,\%$ at every date read, unilateral CVA $0.24\,\%$ and DVA $0.17\,\%$ below ORE's; with the product of the means CVA stood $2.3\,\%$ below and DVA $1.9\,\%$ above (17 November 2026; run by hand, not on every commit). On the monthly grid the engine uses, the same comparison stands at $0.8\,\%$ and $1.0\,\%$: interest inside a monthly step accrues at the rate known when the step begins (\S\ref{sec:ccr}), and ORE fills a step with the rate at its end; each is about half of that from the weekly figure, in opposite directions. \emph{What this does not do.} A currency or digital-asset ticket simulates no rates, so its discounting is today's curve on every path and nothing changes. A request that carries no trade is answered with the generic swap, whose paths carry no account: it stays on the product of the means, and so does the \code{xva\_rates} workbook that rebuilds it. A netting set in several currencies is discounted on the account of the curve its values are reported on. KVA and MVA are sums of expected capital and expected margin and are discounted on today's curve as before. The exposure profiles shown (EE, ENE, PFE) are undiscounted values at the grid dates, so CVA can no longer be read off the chart by multiplying by a discount factor. FCA, FBA and ColVA read the same weighted exposures and have no outside figure of their own; a margined set, a set in several currencies and wrong-way risk together with the weight have none either. The first XVA P\&L and the first counterparty report after the release carry the change as if the market had moved (\S\ref{sec:pnl}): a stored report is not restated.

\textbf{First to default.} The counterparty's default costs the desk only if the desk has not defaulted before it, and the desk's own default is a gain to it only if the counterparty has not defaulted before. With $\tau_{\mathrm{cp}}$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{own}}$ the two default times and $V$ the value of the set to the desk,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CVA} = -\mathrm{LGD}_{\mathrm{cp}}\,\E\big[\mathbf 1\{\tau_{\mathrm{cp}}\le T,\ \tau_{\mathrm{cp}}<\tau_{\mathrm{own}}\}\,D(\tau_{\mathrm{cp}})\,V^+(\tau_{\mathrm{cp}})\big],\qquad
\mathrm{DVA} = \mathrm{LGD}_{\mathrm{own}}\,\E\big[\mathbf 1\{\tau_{\mathrm{own}}\le T,\ \tau_{\mathrm{own}}<\tau_{\mathrm{cp}}\}\,D(\tau_{\mathrm{own}})\,V^-(\tau_{\mathrm{own}})\big].
\end{equation}
The two defaults are taken to be independent of each other (given the market path), so the condition that the other party has not defaulted first is the other party's survival at the moment of default: $\mathrm{CVA}=-\mathrm{LGD}_{\mathrm{cp}}\int_0^T \mathrm{EE}(t)\,D(t)\,Q^{\mathrm{own}}(t)\,\big(-dQ^{\mathrm{cp}}(t)\big)$, and DVA with the parties exchanged. On the grid the default probability of a step is $\Delta Q_i$ and the other party's survival is read at the middle of the step, $\bar Q_i$, the rule $S_i$ below already follows. With constant intensities $\lambda_{\mathrm{cp}},\lambda_{\mathrm{own}}$ the exact probability that the counterparty defaults in a step before the desk is $Q^{\mathrm{cp}}_{i-1}Q^{\mathrm{own}}_{i-1}\,\tfrac{\lambda_{\mathrm{cp}}}{\lambda_{\mathrm{cp}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{own}}}\big(1-e^{-(\lambda_{\mathrm{cp}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{own}})\Delta t}\big)$; $\Delta Q^{\mathrm{cp}}_i\,\bar Q^{\mathrm{own}}_i$ is that to second order in the step. Over five years on a monthly grid the two were measured to agree to $10^{-7}$ at spreads of $250$ and $120$\,bp, $2\times10^{-6}$ at $1{,}200$ and $900$\,bp and $9\times10^{-5}$ with one party at $4{,}000$\,bp, and to $7\times10^{-6}$ with the two loss rates unequal (\code{tests/test\_first\_to\_default.py}, which bounds each a little above its measurement). The model validator derived both figures again from the definition, on paths of the validator's own, by the exact step probability and by simulating the two default times ($30$ million draws): the engine stood within $10^{-7}$ of the exact figure at $85$ and $42$\,bp and $2\times10^{-5}$ at $1{,}500$ and $1{,}000$\,bp, and the simulation within its sampling error of both (17 November 2026). That the two first-to-default probabilities and the chance both survive sum to one is an identity of the midpoint rule and is not evidence for it. The treatment is that of Brigo and Capponi (\emph{Bilateral counterparty risk valuation with stochastic dynamical models and application to credit default swaps}, 2008) and of Gregory (\emph{The xVA Challenge}, bilateral CVA); in Burgard and Kjaer the same weight follows from discounting at $r+\lambda_{\mathrm{own}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{cp}}$. Until version 1.31 neither figure carried the weight: each party was charged as if the other could not default before it (unilateral CVA and DVA). Those two figures are still computed and are in every answer (\code{unilateral}), for a reader who compares with a system that quotes them. What this does not do: the two defaults have no dependence on each other beyond what the market path gives them (where both are tied to the asset they are correlated through it, and only through it), and the two never default at the same instant; the set is closed out at its risk-free value, with no replacement cost that looks at the survivor's credit (the assumption under which Burgard and Kjaer's discounting gives this weight); the weight is read at the date of the default and not at the close-out, so a survivor that defaults inside the margin period of risk of a collateralised set is not counted; and the capital charge for CVA risk (\S\ref{sec:capital}) is unilateral by rule and does not see the weight.

\textbf{The funding adjustment: the cost, the benefit, and why the benefit is on the basis.} Here $s^{\mathrm{ftp}}_i$ is the desk's funding spread over OIS over step $i$, $s^{\mathrm{own}}_i$ its own credit spread over the same step, and $S_i=\tfrac12\big(Q^{\mathrm{cp}}_{i-1}Q^{\mathrm{own}}_{i-1}+Q^{\mathrm{cp}}_iQ^{\mathrm{own}}_i\big)$ the chance that neither party has defaulted, read at the middle of the step. The cost and the benefit are kept as two figures, and they are not symmetric.

\emph{Why.} A desk's funding spread is its own credit spread plus a basis: what its funding costs beyond its credit (liquidity). On what the desk owes, DVA already credits it with its own credit spread: $\mathrm{LGD}_{\mathrm{own}}\,\Delta Q^{\mathrm{own}}_i$ is that spread over the step, times the desk's survival. A funding benefit at the whole funding spread on the same exposure would count the credit part a second time. That a naive funding adjustment beside CVA and DVA counts twice what can be realised once is the point of Morini and Prampolini (\emph{Risky Funding: A Unified Framework for Counterparty and Liquidity Charges}) and of Burgard and Kjaer (\emph{In the Balance}; \emph{Funding Strategies, Funding Costs}); the rule used here is motivated by those papers and is this engine's own, not a formula taken from them. FBA is charged on the basis alone, $\max(s^{\mathrm{ftp}}_i-s^{\mathrm{own}}_i,0)$, step by step. A desk that funds at its own credit spread has no funding benefit beyond its DVA. Where its stated funding spread is below its credit spread the basis is zero, not a negative benefit; DVA is not capped at the funding spread, so on those steps the desk is credited with more than it says it pays for funds. The cost is not reduced in the same way: on what is owed to the desk the credit is the counterparty's, which is CVA, and the desk still funds the whole amount at its whole funding spread.

\emph{How: one reading of a spread curve.} A spread quoted for a tenor covers the whole of $(0,t)$: the credit triangle reads a CDS curve so, $Q(t)=e^{-s(t)\,t/\mathrm{LGD}}$, and what DVA charges over a step is therefore the \emph{forward} spread of that step, $s^{\mathrm{own}}_i=\mathrm{LGD}_{\mathrm{own}}\,\ln(Q^{\mathrm{own}}_{i-1}/Q^{\mathrm{own}}_i)/\Delta t=\big(s(t_i)\,t_i-s(t_{i-1})\,t_{i-1}\big)/\Delta t$. The funding curve is read the same way, $s^{\mathrm{ftp}}_i=\big(s^{\mathrm{ftp}}(t_i)\,t_i-s^{\mathrm{ftp}}(t_{i-1})\,t_{i-1}\big)/\Delta t$ floored at zero, in FCA, in FBA and in MVA, so that the two spreads compared in the basis are spreads of the same step and DVA and FBA together credit the funding spread of each step once. (A first form of this version compared the tenor's own spreads, $s^{\mathrm{ftp}}(t_i)-s^{\mathrm{own}}(t_i)$: on a rising own-credit curve DVA charges more than $s^{\mathrm{own}}(t_i)$, and the two together stood $10$ to $18\,\%$ above the funding spread on the default curves; found by the model validator before release.) A flat spread is itself at every step. Between two quoted tenors the spread is interpolated in a straight line, as the credit triangle has always read a CDS curve, so the forward spread is not flat between tenors and steps at each one: on the default funding curve ($42$ to $62$\,bp from one year to ten) the forward runs from $42$ to $75$\,bp and steps by up to $5$\,bp at a tenor, and beyond the last tenor, where the spread is held, it falls back to that spread. With forwards held flat between tenors instead, the funding cost of a swap-like exposure is $0.6$ to $0.8\,\%$ larger at five years and $0.4\,\%$ at ten, and DVA $0.07\,\%$ smaller (the model validator's figures and the author's, 17 November 2026): a limitation of the interpolation, stated and not mended, because changing it would move CVA and DVA as well. An own-credit curve under which no survival is left is read at the tenor's own spread. With the defaults a desk is shown, the term basis to five years is $55-48=7$\,bp, and the XVA tab prints that figure beside the assumptions as the average basis over five years; a trade of another length is charged on its own steps. Until version 1.30 the benefit was a fixed share ($55\,\%$) of the funding spread whatever the desk's own spread was, a figure that was a default and was not derived; it has been removed.

\emph{What is and is not weighted by survival.} Funding is paid and earned only while the trade is alive, so FCA and FBA carry $S_i$; the curves are the ones the market gives (default independent of the market in this weight, also where CVA and DVA carry wrong-way risk). CVA and DVA carry the other party's survival (first to default, above), so the own-credit part of the benefit, which is in DVA, and the basis part, which is in FBA, are weighted alike: $\mathrm{LGD}_{\mathrm{own}}\,\Delta Q^{\mathrm{own}}_i\,\bar Q^{\mathrm{cp}}_i$ is the desk's own spread over the step times the chance both parties are alive, to second order, and DVA and FBA together are the funding spread of each step credited once under $S_i$ (to $10^{-4}$ on a five-year case with flat spreads of $42$, $85$ and $55$\,bp for the desk, the counterparty and funding; until version 1.31 the two stood $4.8$ to $4.9\,\%$ above it, because DVA carried the desk's survival alone). KVA and MVA are not weighted by survival. Where the desk's own default is tied to the asset the identity is one of the independent figures, not of the charge: DVA with the desk's default on its curve, plus FBA, is the funding spread credited once, and the link's effect on DVA stands on top of it, because FBA reads the curves and does not see the link (on a three-year bought forward under water with the desk's intensity trebling when the asset halves, DVA is $87\,\%$ above its independent figure and DVA plus FBA $66\,\%$ above the funding spread credited once; the model validator's case, 17 November 2026). Survival never rises along a curve: where a steeply inverted curve would have it rise it is held where it stood, in CVA, DVA and the weight alike, so such a step is charged no default and the default probabilities of all the steps sum to one less the lowest survival. No adjustment's sign is set at the end of a run; each is the sum of its steps with the sign those steps have. A swap whose curve is not stored is not simulated on a stand-in: the run is refused and says which curve is missing. Whether a funding adjustment belongs in a price at all is argued in the literature; the engine reports the components and takes no side, and which of them enter the signed P\&L is the firm's policy (\S\ref{sec:pnl}).

\textbf{Currency and digital-asset tickets: the grid, and the exposure a charge reads.} A ticket of a year or less (up to 372 days) is stepped weekly, a longer one monthly. At step $i$ CVA, DVA, FCA and FBA read not $\mathrm{EE}_i$ but the exposure averaged across the step, $\tfrac12(\mathrm{EE}_{i-1}+\mathrm{EE}_i)$ (the same for $\mathrm{ENE}$, and path by path where wrong-way risk links default to the level), with $\mathrm{EE}_0$ today's exposure: today's value less the collateral that value calls, with the first date's initial margin. The exposure of a forward grows like $\sqrt{t}$; read at each step's end on two or three monthly steps it overstated the charge. Measured on a digital-asset forward dealt at fair value, 55\% volatility, 20,000 paths, against daily steps: CVA was 31\% high at one month, 23\% at three, 8\% at six and 9\% at a year uncollateralised, and 23\%, 6\%, 3\% and 2\% under a ten-day margin period. On weekly steps with the averaged exposure it is within 4\% of daily steps at every one of those tenors, both ways. Against continuous time, by closed form on one volatility (the expected exposure of a forward dealt at $K$ is a call's undiscounted value, integrated against the default density), the old rule read 28\% high at one month and 20\% at three, and the engine now reads 2.7\% and 1.7\% low: the averaged rule is a trapezoid, which on a profile that grows like $\sqrt{t}$ reads low by 2.6\% at one month, 0.7\% at three and 0.1\% at a year. A step starts from the set less what was paid on that date, so a settled leg of a package is not charged for one more step. The profiles shown ($\mathrm{EE}$, $\mathrm{ENE}$, PFE) are the values at the grid dates; the replacement cost in KVA, the margin in MVA and ColVA are read at each step's end. A swap's waterfall and the netting-set engine of \S\ref{sec:ccr} keep monthly steps and the exposure at each step's end: the same measurement has not been made there. \emph{Known limitations}: the first step's average starts from an exposure built on today's value alone; a margined KVA still reads about 4\% below daily steps on a six-month ticket and MVA 1\% above; the replication workbook runs monthly steps (its arithmetic is the grid's own; that the grids agree is a test on the engine); a barrier is still watched only at the grid dates.


\textbf{The charge on the ticket.} Every ticket's XVA tab carries, under the trade's stand-alone number, the incremental charge: the counterparty's netting set valued on the credit engine's risk-neutral paths (\S\ref{sec:ccr}) with the trade and without it, the same paths for both runs, the difference per component. That is the number a desk quotes; the stand-alone figure is the trade on its own under a chosen CSA preset. An opened or freshly booked trade shows the marginal form, the set with and without it. The route is \code{POST /api/xva/incremental}; the hypothetical trade is the ticket's booking body with its legs completed as the booking route would store them, so what is charged is what would be booked. \emph{Capital in the set} (since version 1.36): the set's KVA is charged on its SA-CCR rows, each trade in its own asset class and hedging set (\S\ref{sec:capital}), the rows the capital report charges on. Until then the counterparty run took the single-factor interest-rate add-on on the set's gross notional whatever the set held: a six-month EURUSD forward of 10 million was charged $-78$ in the set against some $-1{,}400$ on its ticket, and a forward on ten bitcoin nothing against some $-1{,}100$. With the rows, on one day's market and 4{,}000 paths, the two stand at $-1{,}388$ against $-1{,}409$ and $-1{,}030$ against $-1{,}109$. By hand from the Basel text (CRE52: supervisory factor $4\,\%$, the unmargined maturity factor on the remaining life at the middle of each step, $\alpha=1.4$, no replacement cost) a six-month forward of 11.6 million dollars is charged $-1{,}465.16$, and the engine gives that figure to nine digits (the model validator's calculation, now \code{tests/test\_ccr.py}). Every set's capital charge can change, not only a forward's: a swap alone in a set, and two swaps the same way, are unchanged to the cent; a swaption alone is charged $-8{,}014$ where it was $-13{,}214$, because it enters at its supervisory delta and not at its whole notional; a swap and its mirror are charged nothing where they were charged $-14{,}511$ on their gross notional, because they offset in one hedging set. What the mapping to rows had to assume (a cryptoasset run in the class of Group 2a where its ticket's default is Group 2b, a stand-in rate, a trade it could not map) is in the answer (\code{capital\_notes}), and a set whose rows cannot be built is charged as before and says so. \emph{Where a ticket and its set still differ, measured.} A swap: within $0.2\,\%$ (\S\ref{sec:exposure}). A currency or digital-asset forward: the ticket steps weekly and reads the exposure averaged across each step; the set steps monthly and reads each step's end, so a trade stands in the set until the first monthly date after it has gone. In closed form (a driftless lognormal asset at $8\,\%$ volatility, a flat hazard, no discounting, against a grid of 20{,}000 steps), a forward that settles on a grid date is charged, on the set's reading, $10.6\,\%$ more CVA at six months, $2.9\,\%$ at two years and $1.2\,\%$ at five; on the ticket's, $0.2\,\%$ less at six months and $0.03\,\%$ at two years; the average of each monthly step would be $2.0\,\%$ and $0.3\,\%$ less. A trade that settles in the middle of a step is charged about right by the set and one that settles just before a grid date too little. On the stack's market, where the two runs also read volatility differently, the set's CVA of a six-month EURUSD forward stood $8\,\%$ above the ticket's and of a two-year forward $1\,\%$. DVA is further apart where the two runs draw the asset differently: a digital asset's ticket draws volatility regimes (\S\ref{sec:exposure}) and the set one lognormal volatility ($18\,\%$ on the six-month bitcoin forward), and KVA within $2$ to $7\,\%$ for the grid. Reading each step's average on the monthly grid is not the mend: against ORE it understates a five-year swap's CVA by $2\,\%$, because a coupon is taken out up to a month before it is paid, where the step's end is $0.3\,\%$ off. The mend is to carry each payment to its own date inside the step; it is not built. \emph{A payment under margin} (since version 1.36): where the charges are read on a step's average (a currency or digital-asset ticket, a package), a leg that settles on the step's date leaves the set at the step's start, and under a margin agreement its collateral leaves with it: a settlement is a payment and not a move, so it is neither margined nor left standing as collateral, or as a shortfall, for the step.

\subsection{Wrong-way risk}
On a currency or digital-asset ticket a party's default can be tied to the asset, in the manner of Hull and White (2012): an intensity that is a function of a simulated variable, with a time-dependent level calibrated to the credit curve. The variable (the asset against its forward, not the value of the portfolio) and the form (exponential) are this engine's, so the direction is the desk's input and is not derived from the trade. On path $p$ at grid date $t_i$, with $M_i(p)$ the driver (the asset's forward to settlement over that forward today),
\begin{equation}
h_i(p) = c_i\,M_i(p)^{\,b},\qquad q_i(p) = q_{i-1}(p)\,e^{-h_i(p)\,\Delta t},\qquad \E_p\big[q_i(p)\big] = Q(t_i),
\end{equation}
where $c_i$ is solved date by date so that the mean survival is the credit curve's: the curve is repriced exactly and $b$ only moves default probability between paths. Then
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CVA} = -\mathrm{LGD}_{\mathrm{cp}}\sum_i \E_p\big[E^+_i(p)\,\big(q^{\mathrm{cp}}_{i-1}(p)-q^{\mathrm{cp}}_i(p)\big)\,\tfrac12\big(q^{\mathrm{own}}_{i-1}(p)+q^{\mathrm{own}}_i(p)\big)\big]\,D_i ,
\end{equation}
and DVA likewise with the parties exchanged. A party that is not tied to the asset has the curve's survival on every path, $q_i(p)=Q(t_i)$. Because a tied party's survival is also the weight on the other party's default, a link on one party moves both figures: the one whose default is tied, by the link itself, and the other a little, through the weight (a counterparty that is likelier to have defaulted on the paths where the desk owes takes some of the desk's DVA away; $0.6\,\%$ of DVA and $0.3\,\%$ of CVA in the two cases the tests pin). The independent figure the answer carries for a tied party is its charge with \emph{its} default on the curve and the other party as it is, so what stands between the two is that party's link alone, also when both are tied; a party that is not tied has no such figure. The link's weight was not checked against an outside calculation: the tests state it by hand as the engine does, and check what must follow from the definition (a variable that is the same on every path ties nothing; with a level exposure one link moves neither charge; two links in the same direction make both charges smaller, in opposite directions larger). The desk's input is the multiple $k$ the intensity takes when the asset halves against its forward, $b=-\log_2 k$; $k=1$ is independence and is the calculation above, number for number. The answer carries both figures. The multiple is the desk's judgement of the counterparty and is not calibrated; one constant link per party; funding, capital and margin terms do not see it. \emph{Functions \code{hazard_beta}, \code{path_survival}.}

\subsection{A package of legs as one netting set}
A ticket with several legs on one pair (a strip, a collar, a forward with an option) is simulated as one set (\code{pricing/fx_xva.py}, \code{package_xva}). Every forward of the pair is its own forward today times one driftless lognormal driver, $F_j(t)=F_j(0)\,M_t$: one factor, carry deterministic, so each leg starts at its own price and the legs move together. A forward leg is marked $\pm N(F_j(t)-K)\,D(t,T_j)$ and an option leg is valued in its style's closed form; a leg stays to the first grid date at or after its settlement and is nothing after. The summed paths go through the same waterfall, so exposure nets path by path before any charge is taken, the initial margin estimate follows the legs as they settle, and the add-on is the one above on the legs together. A package of one leg is that leg's own run, draw for draw. One factor means the shape of the forward curve or of the vol term structure moving is not simulated.

\section{P\&L attribution}\label{sec:pnl}
{\raggedright \emph{Module: \code{pnl/explain.py}, \code{pnl/valuation.py}; full description in \code{docs/pnl_attribution.md}.}\par}\nopagebreak

Daily P\&L is attributed by sequential full revaluation: from yesterday's mark on yesterday's market the valuation is moved one thing at a time, in a fixed order (carry, cash, each curve tenor bucket by bucket, FX spot and points), each step's change a row, and what is left between the last step and today's mark on today's market is a \emph{residual} row, shown and never spread. An amendment in the window is one step, $\mathrm{NPV}(\text{new terms}, m_1)-\mathrm{NPV}(\text{old terms}, m_1)$, after the market steps run on the old terms. The rows sum to the change in mark plus cash by construction.

Cash counts the flows that left the mark, those paid in $[d_0,d_1)$: the pricer keeps a flow in the value on its payment date, so a coupon paying today is cash tomorrow (before November 2026 the window was $(d_0,d_1]$ and a payment date's P\&L counted the coupon twice, reversed the next day).

A volatility surface the trade is valued on (an FX option's) is moved after spot and points, bucket by bucket, as a \textsc{vol} step; swaption and cap vols have no step yet and land in the residual.

Booked European swaptions are valued as options in P\&L, VaR, stress and the ladder (Bachelier at the day's nearest ATM grid vol; expiry, strike, payer or receiver and bought or sold from the booked terms, the fixed leg's forward start and rate when the terms are older); until November 2026 they were valued as their underlying swap.

\subsection{Independent price verification}
{\raggedright \emph{Module: \code{backend/ipv}; full description in \code{docs/ipv.md}.}\par}\nopagebreak
The controller verifies the inputs the book is priced on, not the trades: the list is the union over the trades in scope of the curves, funding levels, surfaces and spots they read (\code{pnl.valuation.trade_curves}), and each input is laddered per pillar through the production pricers, one quote moved by one unit (1bp of a rate, 1bp of normal vol, one vol point, 1\% of spot) and the dependent trades repriced.

A pillar is a tenor, a swaption point $(\text{expiry}\times\text{tenor})$ or an FX point $(\text{tenor},\text{ATM}/\text{RR}/\text{BF})$; against an independent mark it carries a gap in the input's unit, a status against the firm's tolerance rule (the most specific rule wins) and a materiality flag ($|s|\cdot\text{tol}$ against the threshold).

An override is a market-data event with author and reason; its first-order P\&L is $s\,\Delta$ and the full revaluation on the overridden market gives the residual as second order. Official P\&L $=$ flash $+$ first order $+$ second order $+$ the day's netted XVA reserve change, signed by the controller once every input is settled and every trade valued; the trading P\&L shows the flash beside it.

\section{Currency and digital-asset forwards}\label{sec:fx}
{\raggedright \emph{Module: \code{pricing/currency_forward.py}, \code{pricing/crypto_forward.py}, \code{chain/canonical.py} (\code{CRYPTO_FORWARD}, \code{CRYPTO_OPTION}).}\par}\nopagebreak

FX spot, forwards and non-deliverable forwards on a currency pair, and forwards on BTC, ETH or SOL against USD, are one product priced by covered interest parity (cash-and-carry): borrow the quote currency, buy the base today and lend it, so the forward must repay the quote-currency interest. Each currency discounts off its own overnight-index curve (USD SOFR, EUR €STR, GBP SONIA, JPY TONAR, CHF SARON, AUD AONIA, CAD CORRA); a digital asset discounts at its funding rate $r_c$ (lending or staking yield, continuously compounded).

With spot $S$ in quote per base, $P_q(T)$ the quote-currency discount factor and $P_b(T)$ the base-currency one, and $t_s$ the spot date (for a USD pair two business days counted in the other currency's centre, then the first day open in both, so a USD holiday on T+1 is ignored; one business day for USDCAD; the valuation date itself for a digital asset, whose quoted spot settles at once),
\begin{equation}
F = S\,\frac{P_b(T)/P_b(t_s)}{P_q(T)/P_q(t_s)},\qquad \mathrm{NPV}_q=\pm N_b\,(F-K)\,P_q(T);
\end{equation}
quoted forward points are measured from the spot date.

A tenor's value date is the spot date plus the tenor: weeks as calendar days, months keeping the day of the month, the end-of-month rule when the spot date is the last business day of its month, rolled modified following on the joint calendar of the two currencies; a digital asset's tenor counts from the trade date and its USD cash settles on the first New York business day on or after it. The calendars (New York, TARGET from the ECB's rule, London, Zurich, Sydney, Toronto, Tokyo) reproduce QuantLib's over three years (\code{tests/reference/test_ref_calendars.py}; until 9 November 2026 five of them lacked their regional days, so a USDCHF spot date could fall on Good Friday). (Before November 2026 the spot was applied from the valuation date, dropping the carry over the spot lag: 5.6 pips on a six-month USDJPY from a Friday.)

\textbf{Non-deliverable forwards.} An NDF pays one net amount against a fixing $X$ of the pair on its fixing date $T_f$, on or before its settlement date $T$: in the quote currency $\pm N_b\,(X-K)$, or, when its record settles in the base currency, the same amount converted at the fixing, $\pm N_b\,(X-K)/X$. The fixing of a currency pair is its spot rate, and a spot rate published on $T_f$ is the price for delivery on that day's spot date $D$. A ticket's fixing date is the latest business day of both currencies whose spot date is on or before the settlement date (two business days before it on most pairs, one on USDCAD), so $D=T$: the rate fixed is the rate for delivery on the settlement date, its expectation is the forward $F(T)$, and $N_b\,(1-K/X)$ of the base currency delivered on $T$ is worth $N_b\,(X-K)$ of the quote currency on $T$ at that very rate. The NDF is then worth exactly what the deliverable forward is, in either settlement currency:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{NPV}_q=\pm N_b\,\bigl(F(T)-K\bigr)\,P_q(T).
\end{equation}
A record that fixes earlier, so that $D<T$, is read at the forward for delivery on $D$, and the amount is carried from $D$ to $T$ on the curve of the currency it is paid in:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{NPV}_q=\pm N_b\,\bigl(F(D)-K\bigr)\,P_q(T)
\end{equation}
paid in the quote currency, and
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{NPV}_q=\pm N_b\,\bigl(F(D)-K\bigr)\,P_q(D)\,\frac{P_b(T)}{P_b(D)}
\end{equation}
paid in the base currency. Both take the expectation of the rate fixed as $F(D)$ although the amount is paid on $T$, and the second takes the base curve's forward discount factor from $D$ to $T$ as known: the covariance of the rate with the discounting over those days is left out. A record that fixes later than the usual day, so that the rate fixed would deliver after the settlement date (a fixing on the eve of the settlement date, or on it, as every ticket before version 0.39.0 of the engine wrote), is read at the settlement date, as the deliverable forward: the carry of the days between, at most the spot lag, is left out (about four pips on a USDJPY fixed the day before it settles). A digital asset's quoted spot settles at once, so there $D=T_f$. (The first build of this, on 14 November 2026, read a currency NDF at the fixing date itself, two days of carry short of the deliverable forward; the model validation of that day found it before it shipped.)

From the fixing date the trade is that one amount (\code{settlement/rule.py}, the rule a digital asset's forward settles by). It settles on the fixing of the source its record names and on no other. No public feed stores a currency fixing: the dealers' benchmark is licensed, and the European Central Bank asks that its reference rates not be used to settle transactions; a desk enters the fixing of the benchmark it is licensed for, and until one is stored the amount is on the stored spot of the fixing date and marked provisional (on the fixing day itself that spot may still be restated, while the sensitivities reported are those of a fixed amount). An amount in the quote currency is discounted from the settlement date. An amount in the base currency is that many units of the base currency delivered on the settlement date, valued as a forward on them at a contract rate of zero, so its value in the quote currency still moves with spot and with both curves until it is paid; at the fixing the two agree. The record decides the currency: tickets before version 0.39.0 of the engine wrote USD on every pair and the settlement date as the fixing date, so an older USDJPY settles in dollars and an older cross, which names neither of its two currencies, in its quote currency, and both are valued as the deliverable forward as they always were. Only the G10 pairs are offered: the fixing lags and calendars of the emerging-market NDF currencies are not modelled. The exposure paths of an XVA run still carry the level to the settlement date.

When a pair carries a quoted forward curve (forward points by tenor, saved like any other snapshot: a feed's, manual or import), the outright at a date is spot plus the points interpolated linearly in time, and the difference to the parity forward is reported as the cross-currency basis; the account chooses, in configuration, whether forwards are priced off quoted points or implied from the two curves, and every quote states which it used.

Beyond the last quoted tenor $T_L$ nothing is held flat. What the last quote shows over parity is read as a rate and carried on:
\begin{equation}
b=\frac{1}{t_L}\,\ln\frac{S+p_L}{F^{\mathrm{par}}(T_L)},\qquad F(T)=F^{\mathrm{par}}(T)\,e^{\,b\,t},\quad T>T_L,
\end{equation}
with $t$ in years from the spot date (ACT/365), $p_L$ the last quoted points in price units and $F^{\mathrm{par}}$ the parity forward of the two curves.

On a currency pair $b$ is the cross-currency basis at the last quote; on a digital asset it is the futures basis, and holding it flat holds the asset's implied rate $r_c-b$ flat from the last quote on. Until v1.13 the last \emph{points} were held, which made a forward beyond the last tenor blind to the carry between the two dates.

Every price names the kind of forward it used: \emph{quoted} (the date is a quoted tenor), \emph{interpolated} (between two, linear in time as above) or \emph{implied} (beyond the last, by the rule above, or parity alone when the pair has no points), with the method in words. The forwards page shows the same on each tenor to ten years, and a tenor can be switched off there: its points stay on the row and its date is priced like any unquoted one. A tenor a venue feed fills beyond the venue's last listed expiry, by this same rule applied in the feed, prices as a pillar and is reported as implied.

\emph{Known limitations}: one number read off the last quote stands in for the whole term structure of the basis beyond it, with no band of uncertainty; that number is what is left over against the parity of the two curves, so an error in a curve is inside it and is carried on; while the points are held a parallel move of either curve leaves a forward beyond the quotes where it was, as it leaves one inside them; a date within seven days of a quoted tenor's round day count is called that tenor (a two-year ticket settles two calendar years from spot, a few days past 730); nothing trades that far out on a digital asset, so the forward there is a model's and is marked so; inside the quoted tenors points are still linear in time; a digital asset's option stops at its last vol tenor (two years) while its forward goes to ten.

For a digital asset against USD this reads
\begin{equation}
F = S\,\frac{e^{-r_cT}}{P_{\mathrm{USD}}(T)} = S\,e^{(r_{\mathrm{USD}}-r_c)T},\qquad \mathrm{NPV} = (F-K)\,N\,P_{\mathrm{USD}}(T).
\end{equation}
A non-deliverable European option is priced with Black-76 on that forward with lognormal volatility $\sigma$:
\begin{equation}
C = P_{\mathrm{USD}}(T)\big[F\Phi(d_1)-K\Phi(d_2)\big],\qquad d_{1,2}=\frac{\ln(F/K)\pm\tfrac12\sigma^2T}{\sigma\sqrt T},
\end{equation}
with the put from parity.

Settlement is in cash against a named settlement price source on a valuation date and time, and the canonical record carries exactly the fields the ISDA Digital Asset Derivatives Definitions name: digital asset, notional amount, strike or forward price and its currency, option type and exercise style, valuation and settlement dates, settlement currency and price source, premium amount, currency and payment date.

\textbf{Status.} Implemented in the engine (\code{pricing/currency_forward.py}, with \code{pricing/crypto_forward.py} as the digital-asset case), priced from parameters and from booked trades on G10 pairs and BTC, ETH, SOL; spot from public reference rates (ECB) and a public exchange feed, the funding rate a desk input; the Excel replications rebuild the FX and the digital-asset forward cell by cell with the engine's NPV, spot delta (\code{FX01}, \code{CRY01}) and rate sensitivities (\code{IR01}; \code{CRYBASIS01} on a digital asset, with the screens' sign) beside the formulas. The canonical on-chain record carries an FX product (pair, both notionals, rate, value date, settlement type, NDF fixing fields) beside the digital-asset one.

\textbf{Replication} (version 1.45). The workbook \code{fx\_forward} is the EURUSD forward as the ticket opens it: settling three months after the pair's spot date, the carry running from the spot date ($\tau$) and the discounting from the settlement date to today ($t$), $F=S\,e^{-r_b\tau}\,P_q(0,\text{spot})/P_q(0,T)$ and the value $N(F-K)P_q(0,T)$. Its formula rows are evaluated outside Excel against the engine: the forward to twelve digits, the value and the three sensitivities to nine. Until this version the book carried from the valuation date and took its settlement date three calendar months from it, and its forward stood about 1.6 pips from the ticket's. The digital-asset book \code{crypto\_ndf} is carried from the valuation date, as the asset's spot settles at once.

Options on the digital assets are the FX option of \S\ref{sec:fxo} with the funding rate as the base rate, cash-settled against the settlement price source the day after expiry; the trade window prices and books them in every style (2 November 2026). A validation note: the first workbook had the discount-factor ratio inverted (forward below spot at positive USD rates); the engine's parity test caught it and both were corrected on 28 October 2026.

\section{FX options}\label{sec:fxo}
{\raggedright \emph{Module: \code{pricing/fx_option.py}, \code{pricing/fx_vol.py}; route \code{POST /api/currency/option/price}; booked valuation in \code{pnl/valuation.py}, exposure in \code{ccr/options.py}, record in \code{chain/canonical.py}.}\par}\nopagebreak

A European option on a currency pair BASE/QUOTE is the right to buy (call) or sell (put) the base currency against the quote currency at the strike $K$, exercised at the expiry time on the expiry date and settled on the settlement date, which for a tenor is the forward's value date; the expiry is the latest date whose spot date is that settlement date (the inverse of the spot rule; where no date has it, around a US Labor Day, the spot lag is counted back and the ticket says so), by delivery of both currencies or, for a non-deliverable option, in the quote currency against a fixing. It is valued with Garman and Kohlhagen (1983), which is Black--Scholes with the base currency as the dividend-paying asset, on the same forward the currency forward uses (\S\ref{sec:fx}: covered interest parity from the spot date, or the account's quoted points), discounted in the quote currency to the settlement date:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
C = N_b\,P_q(T_s)\big[F\,\Phi(d_1)-K\,\Phi(d_2)\big],\qquad P = N_b\,P_q(T_s)\big[K\,\Phi(-d_2)-F\,\Phi(-d_1)\big],\\
d_{1,2}=\frac{\ln(F/K)\pm\tfrac12\sigma^2T_e}{\sigma\sqrt{T_e}},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
with $T_e$ the ACT/365 time to expiry and $T_s$ to settlement, $F$ the forward to settlement and $N_b$ the base notional. A sold option carries the opposite sign. The premium is a cash flow on its payment date, not part of the value once paid; a non-deliverable option has the same value as a deliverable one.

\textbf{Volatility.} A pair's surface is saved as the market quotes it, per tenor: the at-the-money vol, the 25-delta risk reversal $\mathrm{RR}=\sigma_{25\Delta c}-\sigma_{25\Delta p}$ and the 25-delta butterfly $\mathrm{BF}=\tfrac12(\sigma_{25\Delta c}+\sigma_{25\Delta p})-\sigma_{\mathrm{ATM}}$. Across strikes the smile is Malz's quadratic in the call's forward delta,
\begin{equation}
\sigma(\Delta)=\sigma_{\mathrm{ATM}}-2\,\mathrm{RR}\,(\Delta-\tfrac12)+16\,\mathrm{BF}\,(\Delta-\tfrac12)^2,
\end{equation}
which reproduces the three quotes exactly and is solved as a fixed point with $\Delta=\Phi(d_1)$ (the premium-excluded forward delta). ATM, $\mathrm{RR}$ and $\mathrm{BF}$ are interpolated linearly in time between quoted tenors and held flat beyond them; a tenor quoted ATM-only contributes to the level and not to the skew. Conventions stated as limitations: the ATM quote is applied at $\Delta=\tfrac12$ (in this delta convention $d_1=0$, the delta-neutral straddle premium excluded), so the vol read at $K=F$ sits off it: a hundredth of a point at G10 levels, but 0.1 to 0.5 points at one month and up to 0.8 at one year on a 40\% surface with a put skew (the sign follows the skew), and more at higher vols; the smile is a quadratic through three points, so outside the 25-delta strikes it is an extrapolation whose error grows towards the wings (\S\ref{sec:fxo}, digital assets, gives measured sizes); premium-included deltas (the standard for USD-base pairs) are not used. Without a saved surface the pair prices at a flat desk default and the response says so: one table for the price, the exposure runs and the credit engine's factors (\code{fx\_vol.DEFAULT\_VOL\_PCT}: 7.5\% for EURUSD, 55\% for BTC, 70\% for ETH, 90\% for SOL; 10\% for a pair of two major currencies that is not in it and 60\% for any other base, which the engine takes for a digital asset). Until version 1.46 the exposure runs had a table of their own: three currency pairs stood half a point apart in the two (GBPUSD, USDCHF, USDCAD), an unlisted pair of major currencies ran at 9\% in a forward's exposure, and an option on a base that is not a major currency, a digital asset with no surface among them, was priced at the currencies' 10\%. The same table gives the credit engine its factor for a pair with fewer than thirty days of history (\S\ref{sec:ccr}), whether or not a surface is saved, and the vol at which an option with no surface is revalued in P\&L; the daily demo build writes a synthetic surface for the G10 pairs, labelled as such, since no public source quotes FX vols.

\textbf{Risk.} In the desk's taxonomy: \code{spot01} (+1\% spot, repriced; the forward moves with spot, the discounting does not), the equivalent spot position in base units, gamma over a further 1\%, vega per volatility point (repriced), \code{IR01} on each curve (+1bp, repriced: the quote curve raises the forward and lowers the discount factor, the base curve lowers the forward), theta one calendar day later. The ticket shows the forward delta beside them.

\textbf{In the frameworks.} The P\&L explain gains a \textsc{vol} step after spot and points: the pair's surface is moved tenor bucket by tenor bucket, so a day's vega P\&L is a row of its own and only cross terms reach the residual (\S\ref{sec:pnl}). The risk ladder bumps each surface bucket by one volatility point and reports vega by (surface, bucket), kept apart from the rate ladder (\code{docs/market_risk.md}); Volatility is a VaR factor: each surface's quotes (ATM, risk reversal and butterfly per tenor; a swaption's ATM grid point by point) are moved by their observed daily changes beside the curves and spots and every option is repriced in full; a surface is secondary to the curves and spots, held still on a day it has no observation, and the page says how many of the window's days each surface covered. The hosted demo's surfaces have no public source, so their level is scaled day by day by the realised vol of the series they sit on, labelled as such (\code{risk/vols.py}).

On the exposure paths the option is valued before expiry with Garman--Kohlhagen on the path's forward (the path's spot and bonds) at the surface's forward vol for its remaining life and the smile at its moneyness, and after expiry as the payoff fixed on the spot at the expiry grid point and discounted until settlement (\S\ref{sec:exposure}, \S\ref{sec:ccr}).

The canonical record (\S\ref{sec:chain}) carries the fields of the ISDA FX and Currency Option Definitions: pair, option type, exercise style, both notionals, strike, expiry date and time, settlement date and type, settlement currency and fixing source for a non-deliverable option, premium amount, currency and payment date, automatic exercise; buyer and seller are named by LEI so both bookings hash identically.

\textbf{Styles.} (\code{pricing/fx_exotic.py}.) The same dynamics price the other styles a currency desk trades, each with its QuantLib benchmark:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \emph{American}: a Leisen--Reimer binomial tree (801 steps) with early exercise at every node; within 0.02bp of notional of QuantLib's QD+ fixed-point engine and to rounding of its own Leisen--Reimer tree. Where the strike is about 170 standard deviations or more from the forward the tree's probabilities reach 0 or 1 and it has no step sizes; the value there is the best of exercising along the drift $S e^{bt}$: now, at expiry (the European value), or at the interior time where $S e^{bt}=K\,r/r_b$ when there is one (within 0.2bp of notional of QuantLib on both sides of that point; before 9 November 2026 these terms raised an error). No ticket at a market vol reaches it.
\item \emph{Digital}: a fixed quote-currency amount (cash-or-nothing) or a fixed base amount (asset-or-nothing scaled), Reiner--Rubinstein closed forms; to rounding against \code{AnalyticEuropeanEngine}.
\item \emph{Barrier} (up or down, in or out, with a rebate paid at the hit for a knock-out and at expiry for a knock-in): the knock-out value is the integral of the payoff against the survival density of log-spot with one absorbing barrier, built by the method of images with the drift restored by Girsanov, and knock-in $=$ vanilla $-$ knock-out; within $2\times10^{-3}$bp of notional of \code{AnalyticBarrierEngine} across all eight cases.
\item \emph{Double barrier}: the same integral with the two-barrier image series (Ikeda--Kunitomo); within $2\times10^{-3}$bp of \code{AnalyticDoubleBarrierEngine}.
\item \emph{Touch}: one-touch and no-touch from the first-passage and survival probabilities (paid at the hit or at expiry), double no-touch from the two-barrier density, double one-touch as its complement; against \code{AnalyticDigitalAmericanEngine}, its knock-out variant and \code{AnalyticDoubleBarrierBinaryEngine}. The value of one unit paid at the first hit, $(H/S)^{\mu+\lambda}\Phi(\eta z)+(H/S)^{\mu-\lambda}\Phi(\eta z-2\eta\lambda\sigma\sqrt{T})$, is evaluated term by term in logarithms: written as a power times $\Phi$ it lost its first term wherever $\Phi$ underflowed while the power was large ($|b|\sqrt{T}/\sigma$ above about 3.7: up to 4.7\% of the payout at a vol of one or two percent, exact at the vols of the pairs quoted here; found and corrected on 9 November 2026 against a quadrature of the hitting density, which is also what is used when the discount rate is so far below zero that $\lambda$ is not real). The same term is the knock-out's rebate.
\end{itemize}
Barriers are monitored continuously; the vol is the smile vol at the strike, held flat across the barrier (vanna--volga is the next step; QuantLib's analytic barrier engines are flat-vol as well).

On a ticket's exposure paths a barrier is watched on the grid dates and between them: where the level stood inside at both ends $a$ and $b$ of a step that accumulated variance $v$, it crossed $B$ on the way with probability $\exp\!\big(-2\ln(a/B)\ln(b/B)/v\big)$ (the Brownian bridge's crossing probability; with volatility regimes $v$ is the step's own on each path), and one uniform per path and step decides it. Against the continuous closed forms on a six-month knock-out at 55\% volatility, 40,000 paths: the share of paths that touch is within 0.2\% on weekly and on monthly steps, and the knock-out's payoff on the paths is within about 1\% of its price. Watched on the dates alone (until 0.30.0) weekly steps missed 16\% of the touches and paid 63\% more than the price, monthly steps 29\% and 126\%. A corridor adds its two sides' probabilities, capped at one; every option leg of a package reads the same touches, so a knock-in and a knock-out of the same terms remain the plain option. The credit page's netting-set engine still watches on its dates alone. An American option is valued as its European (the early-exercise premium is not simulated), as \code{docs/ccr.md} says. The vol along the paths is the surface's, not one number held: the spot steps with the piecewise forward vols of the ATM term structure, so its log-variance at every grid date is the surface's ATM variance there, and at each date the option is valued at the forward vol for its remaining life with the quoted risk reversal and butterfly at the remaining tenor read at the delta of the path's own forward against the strike (sticky-delta, the FX convention); a touch, which has no strike, takes the forward ATM vol. The same functions (\code{pricing/fx_vol.py}: \code{step_vols}, \code{path_vols}) serve the ticket's stand-alone XVA and the credit engine.

Vol of vol is not modelled: the surface's level does not move along a path, which a stochastic-vol model would add and is on the roadmap with its own validation gate.

The canonical record carries the style, the payout and its currency, the barrier type and levels, the rebate, the touch type, whether a touch pays at the hit, and the monitoring convention. The ticket's scenario tab reprices every style on a grid of spot and vol shocks through the engine itself. The trade's structure in the blotter is its style, so a digital, a barrier and a touch are told apart at a glance and found by search.

\textbf{Digital assets.} An option on BTC, ETH or SOL against USD is the same product with the asset's funding rate $r_c$ as the base rate: the forward is the cash-and-carry one of \S\ref{sec:fx}, $b=r_{\mathrm{USD}}-r_c$, discounting in USD to settlement the day after expiry, settlement in cash against the settlement price source (a non-deliverable option in the ISDA Digital Asset Derivatives Definitions' vocabulary; the canonical record is the \code{CRYPTO_OPTION} product, which keeps its pre-November byte shape for a European and carries the style's terms otherwise). Every style of \S\ref{sec:fxo} prices on the same engine; the reference table carries rows at crypto parameters (spot 65\,000, funding 3\%, vol 60\%).

The desk's taxonomy names the sensitivities: \code{FX01} is the spot delta of a currency pair and \code{CRY01} that of a digital asset (PV for $+1\%$ spot); on a digital asset \code{IR01} is the discounting curve's sensitivity alone and the forward basis's is \code{CRYBASIS01}: the change in value for $+1$bp a year on the basis $\ln(F/S)/T$ at fixed spot and USD curve, a long forward positive, computed as the negative of the $+1$bp bump of the asset's rate (\code{FUND01}, which the API still reports), which agrees with the exact basis bump to about $10^{-4}\,T$ of itself ($T$ in years: $2.5\times10^{-5}$ on a three-month forward, $10^{-3}$ at ten years; on an option the difference is convexity in the forward, $8\times10^{-5}$ on a three-month at-the-money call); it is one flat bucket, not a tenor ladder. On a currency pair both legs are rate curves and the ticket shows the two currencies' \code{IR01} apart.

The surfaces \code{{ASSET}USD_VOL} have the FX shape and, from 9 November 2026, come from a venue: Deribit's public option chain gives each instrument's mark volatility and the forward of its expiry (\code{market_data/deribit.py}; BTC and ETH from their own chains, SOL from the USDC-settled one). Per expiry every strike's mark is placed at its premium-excluded forward call delta $\Phi(d_1)$, the convention of the smile above, and the vol is read linearly in delta at $\tfrac14$, $\tfrac12$ and $\tfrac34$: $\mathrm{ATM}=\sigma(\tfrac12)$, $\mathrm{RR}=\sigma(\tfrac14)-\sigma(\tfrac34)$, $\mathrm{BF}=\tfrac12(\sigma(\tfrac14)+\sigma(\tfrac34))-\sigma(\tfrac12)$, so at a tenor the quadratic returns the venue's marks at those three points; ATM is the 50-delta-forward vol, not the vol at the forward strike.

Across expiries ATM is interpolated linearly in total variance and $\mathrm{RR}$, $\mathrm{BF}$ linearly in time to ten tenors (the FX grid with two and three weeks added, where the listed term structure is steepest); beyond the last listed expiry all three are held flat, and the row's source names that expiry. Between tenors the pricer interpolates linearly in vol as for any pair, so a listed expiry that does not fall on a tenor is not reproduced exactly: within 0.3 of a vol point on the term structure of 9 November 2026, and up to 1.3 points on steeper or event-shaped ones (measured on constructed term structures); inside one week it holds the one-week level, which a steep front end misses by 2.5 to 10 points at four days on the shapes tested. The builder dates an expiry to the hour (08:00 UTC) while the pricer counts whole days, which alone moves a three- to four-day premium by 2 to 3\%.

The forward of each expiry over the venue's index gives an annualised basis $\ln(F/S)/T$, interpolated linearly in time and held flat beyond the last expiry out to the two-year tenor, stored as forward points \code{{ASSET}USD_FWD} (the feed's grid ends at two years; beyond it the pricer carries the last basis on as a rate, as for any pair, and a tenor beyond the venue's last listed expiry is reported as implied); on the account's market forward mode the forward is spot plus those points and the funding rate is the one they imply, while a funding rate named on a forward ticket takes precedence for that price and the forward is then the cash-and-carry one at that rate (the option ticket has no funding field and always sits on the account's forward). \code{CRYBASIS01} keeps its meaning under quoted points: $+1$bp of basis raises each outright by $F\,T\cdot 1$bp, and the ladder moves the points by that.

The response is read under a size cap and a deadline and only finite numbers are used; the marks must be dated within thirty minutes and the venue's index within $5\%$ of the spot fetched in the same run. Within an expiry a mark more than a quarter away from the median of the five marks around it (the worst first, re-read after each removal), or whose delta breaks the order of the strikes, is left out; three stale marks side by side are not seen by this and are left to the bounds; an expiry is left out when it is inside two days, has fewer than six usable marks, has no open interest, does not reach the 25-delta points, gives a level outside sanity bounds or a forward too far from the index; a surface needs two usable expiries. The marks are refreshed several times an hour; when the venue does not answer the last surface stands and its own time stays in the source printed on the ticket.

\emph{Known limitations}: the marks are one venue's, a mark is the venue's model fit rather than a trade, tenors beyond the last listed expiry (about ten months for BTC and ETH, less for SOL) are an extrapolation; the chain's other strikes are not stored, so the smile away from the three points is the quadratic's and no residual of that fit is kept: on test smiles that are not quadratic (an SVI, a parabola in log-moneyness) the 10-delta strike on the skew side priced 2 to 3 vol points, 12 to 19\% of premium, below the smile it was built from, and the 5-delta strike 5 to 9 points below (a third to a half of its premium); reading linearly in delta between marks biases ATM up by about a tenth of a point on a sparse strike grid; \code{CRYBASIS01} under quoted points is exact to the last quoted tenor and, beyond it, to first order (the bump of the last tenor carries through the held basis; until v1.13 the points were held there and it read a third low at three years); the forward points are not yet a factor of the VaR of \S\ref{sec:mr}, and the exposure paths of \S\ref{sec:ccr} still drift a digital asset at the stored flat funding rate rather than at the venue's basis.

\emph{Fixings and settlement.} A digital-asset forward or option settles in cash against the settlement price source its record names, on its valuation date (a forward) or expiration date (an option). A forward's valuation date is its fixing date, a field of the ticket: after the trade date and on or before the settlement date; the settlement date itself for the venue's morning price, one day earlier for a benchmark, unless changed. Before the fixing date a forward that fixes at $T_f$ and is paid at $T_p \ge T_f$ is the forward of the fixing date, discounted from the payment date: $V = \pm N\,(F(T_f) - K)\,D_q(T_p)$, an amount known at $T_f$ and paid at $T_p$. (Until 0.26.0 the forward was carried to $T_p$, overstating the carry by $S\,b\,g/365$ for a gap of $g$ days, and the gap was limited to seven days; there is no limit now.) The exposure simulation of such a forward starts from this value and runs the level on to the payment date, where the amount is in fact fixed from $T_f$: over a gap of days this is small, and it is not a model of a forward that fixes months before it is paid. The counterparty exposure report and the capital built on it, and a forward leg of a package, still carry the forward to $T_p$. Whether a barrier or a touch level traded on the way is not something a fixing can say: the desk records it (the day the level traded, or, once the option has expired, that it never did), and the record is not a term of the signed confirmation. With a touch recorded the option settles on a final amount; before expiry it is valued for what it has become: a knock-in as the plain option, a knock-out and a no-touch as nothing outstanding, a one-touch as its payout (nothing, where it was paid at the hit). Without a record the amount at expiry stays provisional, valued as never touched, with the other amount beside it.

Fixings are stored per pair, date and source: the venue's daily delivery price (its index averaged over the thirty minutes before 08:00 UTC), read once a day and never overwritten by the feed, and a desk's own entry for a benchmark. The venue's price is stored only for a date it can have published and only within 15\% of the template account's stored spot of that date (else of the day before, else the day after); this catches gross errors, not a wrong fixing inside the band, and a genuine fixing after a larger move is refused until an administrator stores it with a reason. A later read of the venue that differs from a stored fixing stores nothing and marks the date disputed. The fixing of a trade is the stored value for exactly its named source; another source's price is never substituted.

A booked valuation names its state. \textsc{live}: before the fixing date, the pricer above. \textsc{fixed}: from the fixing date the amount $\pm N\,(X-K)$, $X$ the fixing, or an option's payoff at $X$, is one cash flow on the settlement date, discounted on the quote currency's OIS curve; its only sensitivities are \code{IR01} $=\mathrm{PV}\,(e^{-1\mathrm{bp}\,t}-1)$ and one day's theta; spot, funding and vega are zero, and an out-of-the-money option reports zero throughout. \textsc{settled}: from the settlement date the value is zero and the amount is reported. The amount is provisional, and says why, in two cases. With no fixing stored for the named source the stored spot of the fixing date stands in for it, valued as the same single cash flow. A barrier or touch is final only when the fixing is itself at or beyond the barrier or the trade records its knock state; otherwise the amount is the one that holds if the barrier was never touched, the amount if it was is reported beside it, and neither is a bound on the other. A touch that pays at the hit, and a knock-out's rebate, recorded as touched have been paid at the hit and nothing is outstanding at expiry.

\emph{Known limitations}: the settlement amount is reported and not yet booked as a cash event, and on the fixing day the P\&L explain shows the move to the fixing as carry rather than as a spot step; the state is by date, so a trade reads fixed from the start of its fixing day, provisional until the fixing is stored; nothing in the application records a knock state or the date of a hit, and a recorded state is not used before expiry; the venue's delivery price and a 4pm London benchmark are different numbers, so a trade's economics depend on the source chosen at booking; the ticket's scenario tab reprices in the browser, for forwards and European options only, with the closed form above (its normal distribution to $10^{-7}$, so to cents with no shock) and the same interpolation of forward points, scaling the quoted points with a spot shock (12.5bp of spot at $\pm10\%$, three months, a 5\% basis): the interpolation, the settlement amounts and a forward's repricing are pinned to the engine's by shared test vectors.

A forward's scenario starts from the engine's own forward $F$ and spot lag $t_s$, both in the pricer's answer, and moves them as the engine would: $F'=F\,(1+s)\,e^{(\Delta_q-\Delta_b)(T-t_s)}$ and $P_q'(T)=P_q(T)\,e^{-\Delta_q T}$ for a spot shock $s$ and parallel shifts $\Delta_q$, $\Delta_b$ of the two rates, the forward carried from the spot date and the discount factor from today; a tenor dragged on the chart adds its change in forward points at the settlement date, moved by the same two factors, and $F'$ is held at no less than 1\% of spot. On a forward implied from the two curves this is the engine priced again, to rounding: with no shock, under a spot shock and under a rate shock (six forwards on flat curves, four of them with a spot lag, in the shared vectors; no non-deliverable forward and no trade settling at spot is among them, and a dragged tenor is not compared with the engine). On quoted points it is the engine's value with no shock only: the tab scales the points with a spot shock where the engine adds the same points to the moved spot (the points times the shock: 2.5 pips on a three-month EURUSD at $+5\%$), and the engine holds a quoted forward when a curve moves, so there the two rate sliders show the carry effect alone, which the tab states; no vector covers quoted points. (Before 9 November 2026 the tab rebuilt a parity forward from the two discount factors counted from today, and the unshocked scenario of a currency pair was off by the carry over the spot lag: 1.6 pips on a three-month EURUSD at 3.9\% against 2.1\%; and it moved the forward under a rate shock over today to settlement, 0.8bp of the forward too far at 100bp and three days.) An option's forward moves by the same rule; its repricing as a whole is not compared with the engine. Its volatility is shocked flat, in vol points, or tenor by tenor of the pair's stored surface: the option is repriced at the volatility it priced at moved by the shock at its own expiry, read on the straight line in time between the two tenors around it (the end tenor's beyond them), so the smile moves in level and not in shape. A package's option legs read the same tenor shocks each at its own expiry.

\emph{Who prices a scenario.} A single trade is priced under a scenario by the engine, at the market the tab works out by the rules above. A spot, a forward or a non-deliverable forward is valued by the pricer's own line, $\pm N\,(F'-K)\,P_q'$, with $P_q'$ the discount factor to the date the amount is paid; a European option by the function the ticket was priced with, on $F'$ and $P_q'(T)$ themselves and the time to expiry the ticket reports, not through rates read back out of them. With no shock both are the ticket's own figures: a forward's to the last digit, an option's to the last digits of its volatility, which travels as volatility points. The browser has the same closed form for these two and shows it for a figure while the engine's answer is on its way, or when none comes (the tab then says so), so that a figure follows a drag; it agrees with the engine to a thousandth of a cent on ten million (until version 1.29 its normal distribution function was a five-term polynomial, good to seven places, and a European option stood up to one unit of the quote currency from the engine on that size). The market itself is the tab's, with the limits stated above: on quoted points a spot shock scales the points and a curve shock moves the forward by its carry, where the pricer would add the same points to the moved spot and hold the quoted forward. A forward paid after its fixing is discounted under a shock by the curve of the currency it is paid in: $P'=P\,e^{-\Delta_q T_p}$ when paid in the quote currency at $T_p$, and $P'=P\,e^{-\Delta_q T-\Delta_b (T_p-T)}$ when paid in the base currency, the base curve carrying the amount over the days from the fixing to the payment, as in pricing. A package of legs is not sent to the engine: its figures are the closed form over its legs, and the tab labels them an approximation.

\emph{An option of another style under a scenario.} The browser has no closed form for an American, digital, barrier, double-barrier or touch option: it is priced under a scenario by the engine alone, and a figure with no answer yet is a dash. The tab works out what the scenario does to the four inputs the pricer takes, by the rules above: the spot $S'=S(1+s)$, the forward to settlement $F'$, the quote discount factor $P_q'(T)$ and the volatility $\sigma'=\sigma+\Delta\sigma$, with $\sigma$ the volatility the ticket priced at, unrounded. The engine values the style there with the function the ticket was priced with, reading the inputs as it does in pricing, $r_q=-\ln P_q'(T)/T_e$ and $b=\ln(F'/S')/T_e$ over the time to expiry $T_e$, and returns the value alone (one valuation a state, where pricing makes eleven for its risk figures). With no shock the four inputs are the ticket's and the figure is the ticket's own, to the last digit. A spot shock of $+1\%$ with nothing else moved is the one-sided revaluation that defines the ticket's value of a 1\% rise in spot (its FX01 or CRY01; not the two-sided delta in units of the base currency), the forward moving with spot and the carry held, and a shock of one volatility point is the one that defines its vega, so those two columns are the ticket's own figures. A rate shock is not the pricer's own bump. A shift $\Delta_q$ of the quote curve moves the discount factor by $e^{-\Delta_q T}$ to the settlement date $T$ and the forward by $e^{\Delta_q (T-t_s)}$, which the pricer reads over the time to expiry: $r_q$ moves by $\Delta_q\,T/T_e$ and $b$ by $\Delta_q\,(T-t_s)/T_e$, so the base rate $r_q-b$ moves by $\Delta_q\,t_s/T_e$; the pricer's bump for IR01 moves $r_q$ and $b$ by $\Delta_q$ exactly and holds the base rate. A shift $\Delta_b$ of the base rate moves $b$ by $-\Delta_b\,(T-t_s)/T_e$ where the pricer's bump moves it by $-\Delta_b$. The scenario's rate columns therefore differ from IR01 times the shock by the settlement and spot lags over the time to expiry: about 7\% for a one-month option that settles two days after it expires, under 1\% beyond a year. Every limit of the forward's scenario above holds here too: on quoted points the tab scales the points with spot and moves the forward with the two rates, where the engine would not. The volatility is one number held at the strike while spot and the forward move (the surface is not read again), and a barrier or touch option is priced on that one volatility as in pricing.

A shock may carry the spot to a barrier or beyond it, where the pricer, which takes a spot inside the barriers, has no value. The option is then valued as touched at that spot, at the limit of the pricer's value as the spot comes to the barrier, with what the touch pays counted in, so the scenario's value does not jump when a shock crosses: a knock-out is worth its rebate, paid then (nothing for a double barrier, which carries none); a knock-in is the plain option at that spot, its own rebate lost; a one-touch is its payout, in full when paid at the hit and discounted from settlement when paid at expiry; a no-touch is worth nothing; a double one-touch is its payout discounted from settlement, as the pricer values it. The terms are checked against the spot the ticket was priced at, which also says on which side a single touch level stands. The tab marks a scenario, and each column of its ladder, in which the level is touched. Tests hold that the ticket's own state gives the ticket's value exactly for each of fourteen options (every style, both barrier directions, in and out, with and without a rebate), that a shocked state inside the barriers is the pricer at those inputs, that the limit of the pricer's value at each barrier (the line through its values two millionths and one millionth of spot inside, read at the barrier) is the touched value to a thousandth of the quote currency on ten million of notional or a payout of 250{,}000, the value a millionth inside being itself within 15, and what each touched style is worth; in the browser, that $+1\%$ of spot and one volatility point move each style by the ticket's own spot delta and vega to 10\% (a tolerance for a ticket that prices itself again during the check, not for a difference in method), and that a point on every tenor of the surface is the flat point. There is no outside reference of its own: the value at a state is the pricer whose prices are compared with QuantLib (\S\ref{sec:quantlib}), and the touched values are the standard limits of those payoffs. Not covered: a package holding such an option is not repriced whole (the tab then shows its first leg alone and says so); the spot delta beside a shock that crosses a barrier is a first-order figure and means little there, which the ladder shows.

Beside the scenario's fixing the tab states what the option would settle by its terms, the reading the settlement rule makes for a digital asset (Fixings and settlement, above): a plain option and a digital by the fixing alone; a barrier or a touch by whether its level traded on the way, which a fixing short of the level cannot say, so both amounts are given, and one alone when the fixing itself stands at or beyond the level.

For an option the tab also draws the pair's surface as the pricer reads it (ATM, risk reversal and butterfly linear in time between the quoted tenors and flat beyond them, the smile in forward call delta, the vol at the strike by the same fixed point), pinned to the engine's reading by the same vectors; a vol shock is drawn as the surface moved in parallel, with the strike left at its unshocked delta. The Methodology tab's workbooks rebuild the European option and the styles at crypto parameters beside the engine's values.

\textbf{Reference.} Against QuantLib's \code{GarmanKohlagenProcess} with \code{AnalyticEuropeanEngine} on the same spot, rates and vol: value to rounding for calls and puts, bought and sold, in and out of the money, to two years; delta in base units to rounding; the repriced spot01, vega and rate sensitivities within the second- and third-order terms of their bumps (\S\ref{sec:quantlib}). The smile reproduces its three quotes at their own strikes. The styles above are in the same table.

\textbf{Replication.} The Methodology tab's workbooks rebuild the European option (Garman--Kohlhagen with its delta and vega), the digitals, the eight single-barrier cases with rebates (Reiner--Rubinstein's terms $A$--$F$), the touches, and the double barriers and double touches (the two-barrier image series summed one row per image) with live Excel formulas beside the engine's values: the closed forms are the independent check of the engine's numerical integration, and agree with it to better than $10^{-6}$ of value. The American option is a tree and has no spreadsheet form; its check is QuantLib's.

\section{Market risk: ladder, VaR, stress, backtesting}\label{sec:mr}
{\raggedright \emph{Modules: \code{risk/sensitivities.py}, \code{risk/var_full.py}, \code{risk/var.py}, \code{risk/stress.py}, \code{risk/backtest.py}, \code{risk/history.py}, \code{risk/vols.py}; full description in \code{docs/market_risk.md}.}\par}\nopagebreak

\subsection{The ladder}
For every live trade and every curve it is valued on, each tenor bucket's quotes are bumped $+1$bp and the trade repriced on the as-of market through the production pricers; the result is key-rate IR01 by (curve, bucket), the buckets (0--1Y, 1--5Y, 5--10Y, 10Y+) the same the P\&L explain uses. Spot deltas are the pricers' PV for $+1\%$ spot: \code{FX01} on a currency pair, \code{CRY01} on a digital asset. On a digital asset \code{IR01} is the discounting curve's sensitivity alone and the forward basis's is \code{CRYBASIS01} (the negative of the asset-rate bump \code{FUND01}), reported beside the IR01 totals and never summed into them. A volatility surface the trade is valued on (\code{{PAIR}_VOL}, \code{{CCY}_SWVOL_ATM}) is bumped $+1$ vol point per tenor bucket, a swaption grid by expiry, and reported as vega by (surface, bucket). A par bump of every quote is a slightly smaller move in zero rates, so the ladder total sits about 2\% below the ticket's IR01, which bumps zero rates; the bucket values agree with a QuantLib rebootstrap to $0.05\%$ of DV01 (\S\ref{sec:quantlib}).

\textbf{At each quoted tenor.} A bucket moves its quotes together, so a two-year position against a five-year one nets inside the 1--5Y bucket and the ladder by bucket cannot tell it from no position. Beside each bucket the ladder therefore carries the points it is made of: every quoted tenor of the curve is bumped $+1$bp on its own, the curve rebuilt and the trade repriced, giving \code{KR01} by (curve, quoted tenor); a surface is bumped $+1$ vol point at one tenor (all of that tenor's rows, as a bucket moves them) and a swaption grid $+1$bp of normal vol at one expiry, giving vega by (surface, tenor). This is the risk at the instrument one would hedge with. A bucket that carries under a hundredth of a trade's largest bucket on the curve is not broken into its points (a five-year swap is priced a little off each of the sixteen quotes of its first year and not at all off the thirty-year one): its own figure stands. Such a bucket is first moved once more, unevenly, its first quote by one basis point, its second by two and so on, and is broken into its points after all if that moves the trade, so that a small net figure made of large points that cancel is not passed over. The points cost a pricing each: on the twelve-trade test book the report takes about twice as long ($2.9$s to $6.1$s). A bucket's figure is still measured by moving the bucket whole by a basis point, so nothing that reads the buckets (the VaR's linear comparison, the limits, the P\&L explain) changes. A point is measured as a slope: the quote is moved by a hundredth of a basis point and the change scaled to one. The reason is that one par quote moved alone by a whole basis point swings the forward rates on either side of it by several basis points in opposite directions, and an option is not linear over that: with each point moved a whole basis point, a cap's points came to $13.5\%$ more than its bucket, and to $0.13\%$ more at a hundredth. So a trade's points add up to its bucket to within the bucket's own convexity over a basis point: $0.03\%$ for a five-year swap, about $1\%$ for a cap, a floor or a collar on the test book. The rate risk of a cap carries the move of its volatility with the forward, because the cap surface is held by distance from the money (\S\ref{sec:caps}); the ticket's \code{IR01} holds the volatility where it was. The response of the curve to one quote is checked against QuantLib: a single quote moved by a basis point, the curve rebuilt and a swap repriced agree to $0.034\%$ of the trade's DV01 (\S\ref{sec:quantlib}), which checks the points where the risk is; a point far smaller than that, at a quote away from the trade's own tenors, takes its sign and size from the curve's interpolation (three builds of the same curve give $+0.4$, $-1.2$ and $-4.5$ at the nine-year quote of a ten-year swap whose DV01 is $8{,}520$) and is not a position. The independent price verification (\S\ref{sec:pnl}) moves each row of a surface on its own; the ladder moves a tenor's rows together, as its buckets do. A funding level has one quote and no points; a point that moves a trade by less than a millionth of its currency is not carried. The trader's home draws a column for a tenor that carries at least $3\%$ of its factor's largest point; the Market risk page lists every one.

\subsection{Historical simulation by full revaluation}
The risk factors are the curve quotes (par rates in basis points, tenor by tenor), the spots (log returns) and the volatility surfaces (every quote of an FX surface: ATM, 25-delta risk reversal and butterfly per tenor; the swaption ATM grid point by point). For each day $i$ of the window today's market $M$ is shifted by that day's observed changes $\Delta_i$ and every trade repriced:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\mathrm{P\&L}_i=\sum_{\text{trades}}\big[\mathrm{NPV}(M+\Delta_i)-\mathrm{NPV}(M)\big]\cdot \mathrm{fx},\\
\mathrm{VaR}_q=-Q_q\{\mathrm{P\&L}_i\},\qquad \mathrm{ES}_q=-\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{P\&L}_i\mid \mathrm{P\&L}_i<-\mathrm{VaR}_q],
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
the horizon scaled by $\sqrt h$. The curves and spots define the window: only days on which every one of them has an observation are used, and a factor with no history is listed on the page, not dropped. A volatility surface is a secondary factor: on a day it was observed its quotes move with the rest, on a day it was not it is held still, and the page states each surface's coverage of the window. The bootstrapped curves are cached per shifted market; beyond a budget of valuations the full revaluation is skipped and the ladder VaR reported, with a note. The ladder VaR is computed alongside as the linear comparison,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{P\&L}_i^{\text{ladder}}=\sum_{c,b}\mathrm{KR01}[c,b]\,\Delta_i[c,b]+\sum_a \mathrm{spot01}[a]\,r_i[a]+\sum_{s,b}\mathrm{vega}[s,b]\,\Delta\sigma_i[s,b],
\end{equation}
and the difference between the two is the convexity and the inside-bucket curve shape the ladder cannot see. Defaults: confidence 99\% (95\% secondary), one day, a 730-day window, sixty observations minimum.

\subsection{History and the demo's surfaces}
\textbf{The demo's rates market is synthetic} (since version 1.38). No interest-rate number of the hosted demo comes from a publisher: not an overnight fixing, not a government yield, not a swap quote. \emph{The recipe, in full.} Each curve is a Nelson--Siegel par curve, $y(t)=\beta_0+\beta_1 f_1(t/\tau)+\beta_2 f_2(t/\tau)$ with $f_1(x)=(1-e^{-x})/x$ and $f_2(x)=f_1(x)-e^{-x}$, in per cent, $t$ in years: $\beta_0$ is the long end, $\beta_0+\beta_1$ the overnight rate, $\beta_2$ the hump. The four parameters $(\beta_0,\beta_1,\beta_2,\tau)$ of each currency were set by hand on 17 November 2026 to look like a market of that time (USD $4.30,\,-0.45,\,-1.00,\,2.0$; EUR $2.90,\,-0.95,\,-0.60,\,2.5$; GBP $4.45,\,-0.50,\,-0.90,\,2.0$; CHF $0.80,\,-0.75,\,-0.30,\,3.0$; JPY $2.40,\,-1.90,\,-0.50,\,4.0$): on that day a curve \emph{is} its parameters, and they follow nothing after it. On every other business day (Monday to Friday; no holiday calendar) three factors have moved the curve, level (added to $\beta_0$), slope ($\beta_1$) and curvature ($\beta_2$). A factor is zero on the set day and walks from it one business day at a time, forwards and backwards: $F_{\mathrm{next\ day\ out}}=\phi\,F_{\mathrm{day\ nearer}}+\sigma\,s\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{day\ out}}$, in per cent, with $\phi=e^{-0.05/252}$ (a mean reversion of $5\,\%$ a year, the policy of the exposure engine, \S\ref{sec:ccr}), a daily $\sigma$ of $3.5$, $2.0$ and $1.5$\,bp for the three factors, and a scale $s$ per currency (USD $1.00$, EUR $0.85$, GBP $1.05$, CHF $0.60$, JPY $0.50$), also set by hand. The shock is $\varepsilon(\mathrm{curve},\mathrm{factor},\mathrm{day})=\sqrt{0.5}\;n(\texttt{all},\mathrm{factor},\mathrm{day})+\sqrt{0.5}\;n(\mathrm{curve},\mathrm{factor},\mathrm{day})$, so every currency shares half of each shock's variance; $n(\cdot)$ is a standard normal made from the SHA-256 hash of the text \code{rijeka synthetic rates 1|key|key|...}, where the factor's key is one of the words \code{level}, \code{slope}, \code{curvature}, the curve's key is its id (\code{USD\_SOFR}, \code{EUR\_ESTR}, \code{GBP\_SONIA}, \code{CHF\_SARON}, \code{JPY\_TONAR}) and the day is written as its proleptic Gregorian ordinal, so the level shock all currencies shared on 18 November 2026 is hashed from \code{rijeka synthetic rates 1|all|level|739938} and the dollar curve's own from \code{rijeka synthetic rates 1|USD\_SOFR|level|739938}: $u_1=(\text{first 8 bytes, big-endian}+1)/(2^{64}+1)$, $u_2=(\text{next 8 bytes})/2^{64}$, $n=\sqrt{-2\ln u_1}\,\cos(2\pi u_2)$. A weekend carries Friday's curve. The pillars are the 33 tenors of the curves page, the overnight point read at $1/365$ of a year; a stored quote is the function's value rounded to four decimals (the function itself does not round, and reads a maturity below $10^{-6}$ of a year as $10^{-6}$). A curve is therefore a pure function of the currency and the date: today's quotes, any day's overnight fixing and a history of any length are the same function and cannot disagree (\code{market\_data/synthetic\_rates.py}; the recipe, with the seed, is also on the public endpoint \code{GET /api/market-data/demo/sources}; a test rebuilds a shock from this text alone and a factor's first two steps by hand). The curve ids keep the names of the conventions each is bootstrapped with (day count, calendar, settlement lag); the default quotes shown on the curves page before the API answers are the set day's curves, written by a script from the same recipe (\code{backend/scripts/synthetic\_rates\_defaults.py}; a test fails if the file is not what the script makes). \emph{What it does, measured.} Over the four years to the set day a ten-year rate moved $3.6$\,bp a day in USD, $3.1$ in EUR, $3.7$ in GBP, $2.1$ in CHF and $1.8$ in JPY, with a correlation of $0.50$ between the daily changes of USD and EUR; the three-month rate's annual volatility, which the real-world exposure calibration reads, was $65$, $56$, $66$, $38$ and $32$\,bp. A level wanders from the set day like a slow random walk: about $\sigma s\sqrt{n}$ after $n$ business days ($80$\,bp in two years at scale one), at most $\sigma s/\sqrt{1-\phi^2}=176$\,bp times the scale in the long run. Over twenty years, ten each side of the set day, the quotes ran from $1.5$ to $5.9\,\%$ in USD and from $-1.6$ to $1.2\,\%$ in CHF, and all 2{,}610 curves sampled (five currencies, fortnightly) bootstrapped, with one-month forwards between $-1.6$ and $5.9\,\%$. The model validator rebuilt a ten-year rate from an implementation of the formula of the validator's own, on the engine's shocks, to fifteen digits (18 November 2026, on the first version of the factors; the mean reversion was then far too fast, about $3.8$ a year, and was changed to the present one on the validator's finding). On the present version the validator wrote an implementation from this paragraph alone, before reading the code, and reproduced six rates bit for bit (the dollar ten-year on three dates either side of the set day, the euro overnight, the yen ten-year two years back, the franc five-year in June 2027), having had to guess the hash's key words, which are printed above since. \emph{What follows from it.} A price, a sensitivity or an adjustment of the demo is exact arithmetic on a market that is not one. The rates history that VaR, the backtest and the real-world calibration of counterparty exposure read is this generator's: Gaussian daily moves with no jumps and no meeting dates, around hand-set levels of one day. Those figures describe the generator and not any market's tail. The Swiss franc curve sits near zero and its short end is below zero about as often as above. The exposure engine's real-world mean reversion is $0.05$ a year by policy, which is the generator's; the two-year AR(1) estimate shown beside it is at its upper bound of $0.50$ on every window measured, as it is on any near-random walk of that length, which is why the policy and not the estimate is used. The Federal Reserve's published projections on the home page are the Fed's; the implied path drawn beside them is read from the synthetic curve and says so. Reports stored before the switch (a day's P\&L, a VaR, a backtest pair) were struck on the earlier market and are not comparable with those after it; the stored rates history is replaced as a whole when it is rebuilt, so no window mixes the two. Until version 1.38 the curves were built daily from published overnight fixings and government par yields with a stated spread schedule; the firm holds no licence for any benchmark, and the administrators of some require one to build a curve from or to value with. Spots are still read from public sources: ECB reference rates for currency pairs, a venue's daily closes for digital assets; each day's values are stored as the template account's snapshot, so the daily rebuild extends the series.

No public source quotes implied volatility for rates or currency pairs, so those surfaces are synthetic in shape and market-driven in level: each day the parametric surface's ATM is scaled by $\sqrt{\sigma^{\text{real}}_{\text{tenor window}}/\sigma^{\text{real}}_{\text{series}}}$ from the stored spot series (an FX pair, a digital asset) or from the 5Y par rate's changes (the swaption grid), clipped to $[\tfrac12,2]$, risk reversals and butterflies as quoted; every such row is labelled. (The one-week row is the exception: its window of five changes is below the estimator's minimum of ten, so its scale is always one and the row does not move day to day.) A desk with a feed stores its own surfaces under the same ids.

The digital-asset surfaces are a venue's marks from 9 November 2026 (\S\ref{sec:fxo}) and a built row never replaces one; the dates before the venue's first surface are rebuilt from it, $\mathrm{ATM}(t,d)=\mathrm{ATM}^{\text{venue}}(t)\cdot s(t,d)/s(t,d_0)$ with $s$ the scale above and $d_0$ the first venue date, its risk reversals and butterflies held, so the series has no step on the day the source changed (the ratio of two clipped scales lies in $[\tfrac14,4]$, and the level of the whole back-history depends on the scale on $d_0$). That rebuild takes effect when the history is next backfilled; until then a window containing $d_0$ samples the change of source as one day's move in vol, and the VaR of a book with digital-asset options is to be read with that in mind.

\subsection{Stress}
Named scenarios by full revaluation: every trade repriced on the shifted market. Rates scenarios shift quotes in basis points, parallel or by bucket (parallel $\pm25$, $\pm100$bp; steepener and flattener $\pm25$ short against long); FX scenarios move the non-USD asset by a percentage, a USD-first quote the other way (USD $\pm10\%$); digital assets $\pm30\%$; a combined risk-off (rates $-50$bp, USD $+5\%$, digital assets $-30\%$); and the \textsc{vol} scenarios move the FX surfaces' ATM rows by a number of points (larger for the digital assets) and the swaption grid by bp of normal vol. A rate scenario leaves the surfaces alone. A trade whose pricer cannot value the shifted market is listed under the scenario, not summed as zero.

\subsection{Limits and backtesting}
A limit names a metric (\code{VAR_99_1D}, \code{ES_99_1D}, \code{IR01}, \code{IR01:<curve>}, \code{KR01:<curve>:<bucket>}, \code{SPOT01:<asset>}, \code{NPV}, \code{STRESS:<scenario>}), a scope and a value; utilisation is $|\mathrm{value}|/\mathrm{limit}$ with a warning level and a breach at 100\%, evaluated on every member's book after the daily run. Backtesting pairs every stored P\&L date with the VaR standing the evening before; an exception is a day whose loss exceeded it, on the clean (positions held at the previous close), hypothetical (clean without carry) or dirty P\&L. The count is read two ways: the Basel traffic light from the cumulative binomial at the stated confidence (green below 95\%, yellow to 99.99\%, red beyond: at 250 days and 99\%, 0--4 / 5--9 / 10+), and Kupiec's proportion-of-failures test, $\mathrm{LR}=-2\ln[(1-p)^{n-x}p^x]+2\ln[(1-x/n)^{n-x}(x/n)^x]\sim\chi^2_1$, two-sided, so too few exceptions is also a finding. Sixty paired days are needed before the page says anything.

\textbf{Known limitations.} One window, equally weighted (no filtered historical simulation yet); swaption and cap vol moves are in VaR through the ATM grid only, and cap vols have no P\&L step (\S\ref{sec:pnl}); the smile's own moves enter the ladder VaR through vega $\times$ ATM only.

\section{Counterparty credit: netting sets and real-world exposure}\label{sec:ccr}
{\raggedright \emph{Modules: \code{ccr/simulate.py}, \code{ccr/valuation.py}, \code{ccr/options.py}, \code{ccr/calibrate.py}; full description in \code{docs/ccr.md}.}\par}\nopagebreak

\subsection{Two measures}
The same model is run under two parameter sets and every number is labelled with its measure. \emph{Risk-neutral}: the Hull--White parameters of \S\ref{sec:exposure} calibrated to the swaption surface, each spot stepping with the ATM term structure of its pair's surface (a desk default, labelled, for a pair without one), the drift from the curves; its outputs are the XVA reserves and belong to the P\&L. \emph{Real-world}: volatilities measured from two years of public history (daily changes of each currency's 3M rate, log returns of each spot), a mean reversion set by policy ($a=0.05$; two years of daily data cannot identify it, and the estimate is shown beside it), zero drift by default (the forward slope is a risk-neutral artefact, the historical drift the firm's alternative), and the correlation of all factor shocks from the same history; its outputs are EE, EPE, PFE and the limits.

\subsection{Joint simulation}
One Hull--White factor per currency and one lognormal factor per spot on a monthly grid to the netting set's last maturity, Brownian motions correlated by the calibrated matrix (identity in the risk-neutral run). Grid times are each grid date's distance from the as-of date in $365.25$-day years, the clock every trade date is read on. The short rate is $r(t)=f(0,t)+\varphi(t)+x(t)$ with $x$ the driftless Ornstein--Uhlenbeck deviation and $\varphi(t)=\frac{\sigma^2}{2a^2}(1-e^{-at})^2$; bonds are affine in $x+\varphi$ and reproduce today's curve,
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
P(t,T)=\frac{P(0,T)}{P(0,t)}\exp\!\Big(-B(t,T)\,(x_t+\varphi(t))-\frac{\sigma^2}{4a}B(t,T)^2(1-e^{-2at})\Big),\\
B(t,T)=\frac{1-e^{-a(T-t)}}{a}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
(Until November 2026 the exponent was evaluated on $x_t$ alone, which priced every bond high by $e^{B\varphi}$: 8bp at five years, 27bp at ten for $a=3\%$, $\sigma=85$bp; the martingale check $\mathbb{E}[D(t)P(t,T)]=P(0,T)$ in the reference suite pins it.) Spots follow $S_t=S_0\exp((\mu-\sigma^2/2)t+\sigma W_t)$, $\mu$ the rate differential or zero. \textbf{Historical sampling} is the default real-world measure: each monthly step is a block of 21 consecutive observed daily changes from the calibration window, the block's start drawn uniformly and the same block for every factor; the block's sum is the step's increment, in the Hull--White recursion $x_i=x_{i-1}e^{-a\Delta t}+\sum\Delta r$ in place of $\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}\,z$, and the step's log return for a spot; the panel is demeaned so the drift is the stated one. This keeps the window's fat tails, its volatility clustering within a block and the realised co-movement of rates and spots without a correlation matrix; Gaussian shocks are the alternative, chosen per firm, and the fallback when a factor has no history on common dates.

\subsection{Valuation on the paths, netting and the CSA}
Every trade of a counterparty is valued on the same paths and summed per path and date, so netting is exact. A swap is valued from the path's bonds: the fixed leg is $\sum_j N c\,\tau_j\,P(t,T_j)$ over the coupons not yet paid, and the floating leg is the sum over its periods not yet paid, a period $[s,e]$ paid at $p\ge e$ being worth
\begin{equation}
N\Big(\frac{P(t,s)}{P(t,e)}-1\Big)P(t,p)\ \ (t<s),\qquad N\Big(\frac{G(s,t)}{P(t,e)}-1\Big)P(t,p)\ \ (s\le t<e),\qquad N\big(G(s,e)-1\big)P(t,p)\ \ (e\le t<p),
\end{equation}
where $G(a,b)$ is what one unit placed at $a$ has grown to by $b$ on the path: the period's interest accrued so far, with the unit. On the grid $G$ is rolled at each step's own bond, $G(t_k,t_{k+1})=1/P(t_k,t_{k+1})$, and inside a step at the forward seen on the grid date before, so the discounted growth is a martingale and the rate's variance inside one step is not in it; a span before the valuation date has no path behind it and grows at today's rate for that length (the fixings are not read here). With $p=e$ the first two are $N(P(t,s)-P(t,e))$ and $N(G(s,t)-P(t,e))$. The floating coupon is a cash flow of its step like a fixed one, the path's own amount. Under a margin agreement the value a margin period back is drawn by a bridge between grid dates whose noise is the spread of the step's change; in a step that holds a coupon that spread is the coupon's and not the market's, so the bridge is drawn on the value with its payments taken out and the payments made by each date are put back: a coupon is not a market move over the margin period. (Until version 1.32 the bridge was drawn on the value as it stood: at the last payment of a yearly swap, when nothing is left to move, the margined expected exposure stood at tens of thousands on ten million where it is now about nothing. The model validator, 17 November 2026; it held for fixed coupons as well.)

Until version 1.32 the whole leg was $N(P(t,T_s)-P(t,T_N))$ with $T_s$ read as $t$ once the first period had begun, as if it reset at $t$, and $T_N$ the last \emph{payment} date. The first left out the interest accrued since the period's start: on a receive-fixed par swap with both legs yearly, worth nothing on average, the mean value stood at $+34{,}628$ a month into the year and $+375{,}222$ eleven months in on a notional of ten million at the par rate of a flat $4\,\%$ curve ($4.025\,\%$), and fell back at each payment. The second overvalued a leg that pays two days after each period ends, the ticket's own swap, by about $N(1-e^{-r\delta})$ with $\delta$ the lag: $2{,}319$ on ten million at the start of a five-year swap, less as its coupons are paid. The check that pins both does not use these formulas: the value at each grid date, discounted along the path, averages to today's value of the coupons not yet paid at that date, read off today's curve coupon by coupon; the discounting is done by the engine's cash account and again by the trapezoid of the short rate, for yearly, half-yearly and quarterly floating legs, both directions, a two-day payment lag, a period ended and not yet paid, a swap begun ten days ago and one beginning in ten days, leverage and a spread, at every grid date, and to the dollar on the valuation date, where there is no sampling; and the accrual is compared path by path with the integrated short rate, since a mean cannot see the spread across paths that PFE is made of (\code{tests/test\_ccr.py}). The model validator derived the correction again with an account of the validator's own (17 November 2026): the old value stood $53$ to $1{,}800$ standard errors from today's curve between coupon dates, the new within $1.6$. Where the two legs pay at different frequencies the saw-tooth that remains is real: three quarters of floating interest have been paid and the year's fixed coupon has not. For a swap already running, the value at the start of the paths now carries the floating interest accrued to date: $-201{,}000$ on ten million for a yearly leg half a year in (the validator's figure), where it carried the fixed leg's accrual alone. Not done: that past part is at today's rate, not from the fixings; no library reference for a swap's value between coupon dates stands beside the martingale check. Forwards are valued as $\pm N(S_t\,\mathrm{DF}_b/\mathrm{DF}_q-K)\,\mathrm{DF}_q$. Options are on the paths too: a cap's or a floor's period is Black's formula on the growth of one unit over the period at the variance the paths' own model gives it, then the realised payoff discounted to payment (\S\ref{sec:exposure}, equations \eqref{eq:growthvar} and \eqref{eq:capletpath}); a cap, a floor and a collar are in the set since version 1.43, where they were listed as not simulated; a European swaption is Bachelier on the path's forward swap rate at the normal vol the paths' own Hull--White model implies for it (the Jamshidian price inverted through Bachelier, so value before expiry and exercise at expiry are one model and exposure does not step at expiry), then the underlying swap where it was in the money (physical settlement); an FX or digital-asset option is valued on the path's forward at the surface's forward vol for its remaining life and the smile at its moneyness (sticky-delta), its payoff fixed at the expiry grid point and discounted to settlement; barriers and touches carry which paths have knocked, monitored at the grid points seen so far; an American option is valued as its European. Anything else is listed as not simulated, never valued at zero. The CSA is the engine of \S\ref{sec:exposure}: variation margin against the value lagged by the margin period of risk (a Brownian bridge inside the step), initial margin when the record says so, residual $E^+=\max(V-C-\mathrm{IM},0)$.

\subsection{Measures and limits}
$\mathrm{EE}(t)$ the mean of $E^+$; $\mathrm{PFE}_q(t)$ its $q$-quantile over paths at 95, 97.5 and 99\%; $\mathrm{EPE}$ the time average of $\mathrm{EE}$ over the first year; the peak $\mathrm{PFE}_{95}$ and its date; the largest $\mathrm{PFE}_{95}$ in each tenor bucket; current exposure $\max(V_0-C_0,0)$. Credit limits are set on \code{PFE_PEAK:<cpty>}, \code{PFE:<cpty>:<bucket>}, \code{EPE:<cpty>}, \code{CE:<cpty>} and \code{CVA:<cpty>} with the same utilisation and breach rules as the market-risk limits. The reserves come from the risk-neutral paths through the waterfall of \S\ref{sec:exposure} with the counterparty's spread (a desk default of 85bp and 40\% LGD until the record carries its own).

\textbf{Known limitations.} The bridge noise is drawn independently per step, so the 99\% tail is jagged month to month while the 95\% profile is smooth; one factor per currency, so no curve twist in the exposure; the surface's level does not move along a path (its term structure and smile are applied, \S\ref{sec:fxo}; vol of vol is not simulated); the risk-neutral spot paths of a pair without a surface use a desk default vol, labelled; wrong-way risk and stressed EEPE are on the roadmap.

\section{Collateral regimes and counterparty capital}\label{sec:capital}
{\raggedright \emph{Modules: \code{pricing/collateral.py}, \code{pricing/sa_ccr.py}, \code{pricing/capital.py}, \code{ccr/saccr_map.py}; full description in \code{docs/capital.md}. Built from the public texts named at each formula; no third-party material.}\par}\nopagebreak

\subsection{Collateral classes and the two CSAs}
A CSA (\S\ref{sec:exposure}) says whether collateral is exchanged; a \emph{regime} says what it is and what each rulebook makes of it. A collateral class carries its recognition under the credit-risk-mitigation rules (CRE22.34--22.45; SCO60.94 for tokenised and crypto assets), its supervisory haircut by residual maturity (CRE22.49), its eligibility as initial and variation margin (CFTC \S23.156), its liquidity level (LCR30) and its cryptoasset group (SCO60). A regime is a CSA, an eligible set, the compositions posted and received, and three labelled switches: close-out enforceability, the initial-margin eligibility state of a conditional class, and the margin period of risk (the non-cleared floor or the cleared floor for atomic settlement). From one regime the engine derives two CSAs:
\begin{align}
\text{regulatory:}\quad & C_{\mathrm{reg}}=C\,\big(1-h\big),\qquad h=1-\textstyle\sum_i w_i\,\mathrm{rec}_i\,(1-h_i),\\
\text{economic:}\quad & C_{\mathrm{eco}}=C \text{ when enforceable, else } 0 \text{ (gross exposure)},
\end{align}
with $w_i$ the composition weights, $\mathrm{rec}_i\in\{0,1\}$ the recognition and $h_i$ the haircut; the regulatory reading is margined if and only if recognised variation margin is exchanged and enforceable. The regulatory CSA feeds SA-CCR, CVA capital and the leverage exposure; the economic CSA feeds CVA, DVA, FCA, FBA, MVA and ColVA, with the pickup on posted margin the yield of its eligible part. The all-recognised regime reproduces the plain CSA field by field, so every number of \S\ref{sec:exposure} is the cash-and-government-securities column.

\subsection{Instruments, schedules and the carry over OIS}
A class is a rulebook; an \emph{instrument} is the asset: a code, its class, currency and issuer, its settlement rail (bilateral, or on chain with a token address), its yield source and an optional negotiated haircut. The yield source is a curve's short rate plus a spread (cash in a currency on its OIS; government paper on the same curve until a government curve is loaded; a tokenised fund share at OIS less its expense ratio), a fixed rate the firm enters, or none (a payment stablecoin, a cryptoasset). The public seed is cash in three currencies, Treasury bills and notes, Bunds, Gilts, three payment stablecoins, a generic tokenised money-market fund share, a generic tokenised Treasury-bill claim and three cryptoassets; a firm adds its own instruments under its own names.

What the engines use is each instrument's \emph{carry over OIS}: a curve instrument on its currency's OIS is its spread, a fixed rate is the rate less the OIS one-year zero of the account's latest market, nothing is minus that OIS.

A \emph{schedule} per counterparty (the credit support annex's eligible-collateral list, versioned by effective date) names the admitted instruments, whether each is admitted for variation and for initial margin, a negotiated haircut and a concentration limit, the composition posted and received, and the rail. The regime of \S\ref{sec:capital} is derived from it: the eligible classes are the instruments' classes, the compositions aggregate by class, the on-chain rail sets the on-chain margin period, each class's yield is the weighted carry of the instruments posted in it, and the two CSAs carry the weighted carries of the posted and received compositions, which ColVA integrates.

A cash schedule on the bilateral rail reproduces the class-level regime field by field (\code{tests/test_collateral_instruments.py}); on the ticket the trader may re-collateralise the same trade in any instrument on the firm's list, and the on-chain comparison runs the same paths under atomic settlement with the chosen collateral, so the difference is the terms alone.

\subsection{Capital, CVA capital, leverage}
$\mathrm{RWA}=\mathrm{RW}\cdot \mathrm{EAD}$ and $K_{\mathrm{CCR}}=8\%\,\mathrm{RWA}$ with the standardised risk weight by counterparty type and grade (sovereigns CRE20.7, banks CRE20.18, corporates CRE20.42--20.43; broker-dealers as banks when prudentially supervised, else as corporates, CRE20.40; an explicit override on the record, labelled). CVA capital follows the reduced basic approach,
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\mathrm{SCVA}_c=\tfrac{1}{1.4}\,\mathrm{RW}_c\,M\,\mathrm{EAD}\,\mathrm{DF}(M),\qquad \mathrm{DF}(M)=\tfrac{1-e^{-0.05M}}{0.05M},\\
K_{\mathrm{CVA}}=0.65\sqrt{(0.5\textstyle\sum_c \mathrm{SCVA}_c)^2+0.75\sum_c \mathrm{SCVA}_c^2},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
(MAR50.14--50.15) with $\mathrm{RW}_c$ by sector and grade (MAR50.16, Table~1). The waterfall's KVA becomes $-\kappa\sum_i\big(K_{\mathrm{CCR}}(t_i)+K_{\mathrm{CVA}}(t_i)\big)\Delta t\,D_i$ with $M(t)$ the remaining maturity; without a CVA weight it is the KVA of \S\ref{sec:exposure} exactly. The leverage exposure is $1.4\,(\max(V-C_{\mathrm{cash}},0)+\mathrm{AddOn})$ with only cash variation margin reducing it (LEV30); the HQLA value of the margin posted uses the Level~1, 2A and 2B haircuts (LCR30). The cost difference between two regimes is quoted as a running spread over the trade's annuity or an upfront spread of notional.

\subsection{The book under regimes: attribution by channel}
Every netting set is simulated once on the risk-neutral paths and re-collateralised step by step from the CSA as booked to an on-chain CSA under today's rules (zero threshold and MTA, CRE52.18--52.19; daily margining, CRE52.50; the dispute doubling removed, CRE52.51; recognised collateral and enforceable netting, CRE22.34 and SCO60.94; eligible initial margin in the asset held at its yield, CFTC \S23.156), then to the forward-looking states (the cleared-floor margin period of risk; a tokenised sovereign claim eligible as initial margin; Level~1 liquidity). Each step changes one thing in the previous step's CSA and the difference it makes is that channel's attribution, sequential as the P\&L explain of \S\ref{sec:pnl}; the steps sum to the columns exactly. The record's margin period of risk is $9+N$ business days for re-margining every $N$ days, floored at 10 and doubled while disputed. A record already on cash and government securities shows zero in the collateral step (parity, not superiority); the initial margin estimate moves only with the margin period of risk; market-risk capital is not a function of any step. \emph{Module: \code{ccr/capital_report.py}; route \code{/api/capital/portfolio}.}

\textbf{Known limitations.} Initial margin is the desk's own amount or the engine's estimate at the model's current volatility (\S\ref{sec:exposure}), not a regulatory margin model; supervisory deltas are held fixed along the path; the leverage and liquidity figures are the trade's stock, not the ratios; a digital asset's cryptoasset group is the engine's default unless the desk sets it (\S\ref{sec:exposure}).

\section{On-chain bilateral confirmation}\label{sec:chain}
{\raggedright \emph{Modules: \code{chain/canonical.py}, \code{chain/signing.py}, \code{chain/attestation.py};\newline the registry contract \code{chain/src/TradeConfirmationRegistry.sol}; the verifier \code{chain/verify/index.html}.}\par}\nopagebreak

\subsection{The canonical record (schema v2, and v3 for an option on a rate)}
Both parties sign one record that either can derive alone from its own booking. The record is keyed by the unique transaction identifier, names the two parties by LEI in sorted order, names the payer and receiver LEI of every leg, the buyer and seller of every embedded option and the payer and receiver of every custom cashflow, and orders legs by content. It contains only the economic terms: no internal references, no book or desk, no status, no timestamps, no party names. Serialisation rules, which a verifier in any language must reproduce exactly: JSON, UTF-8, keys sorted at every level, separators \code{,} and \code{:} with no whitespace; numbers as decimal strings without exponent, trailing zeros or negative zero; dates ISO-8601; nulls and booleans preserved; strings unchanged. The trade hash is $h=\mathrm{keccak256}(\text{bytes})$. The schema version is inside the payload so a confirmation is always re-derived under the version it was signed with.

\emph{An option on a rate: schema v3} (since version 1.44). A swaption, a cap, a floor and a collar are signed under version 3, which is version 2 with one \code{option} block; every other product is signed under version 2 and its bytes are as they were. For a swaption the block names the buyer and the seller by LEI, the right (payer or receiver: what the buyer does with the fixed rate if it exercises), the exercise style, the expiry date, a Bermudan's later exercise times as booked (in years from the trade date; none means the expiry date only), the strike, physical settlement, and the premium: its amount, currency and payment date. The legs are the swap delivered on exercise, with their own effective and termination dates, and each names the payer it will have if the buyer exercises: the buyer of a payer swaption pays the fixed rate, whichever side booked the trade. The trade block of a swaption holds no effective or maturity date: the option has its expiry and the swap its own dates. The strike is the fixed rate of the delivered swap as the leg holds it, to eight decimals; a booking whose two copies of it differ, one with no expiry, or one whose swap starts before the option expires, is refused a record. For a cap, a floor and a collar the strikes and the buyer and seller of each option were already on the leg (version 2); version 3 adds the premium, held unsigned with who pays it and who receives it (a collar whose floor is worth more than its cap pays the side that bought the cap). The buyer of a cap, a floor or a collar is read from the option on the leg (a collar's from its cap), which the valuations read; a booking whose own side says otherwise, whose leg holds no such option, or whose collar has both options bought by one side, is refused a record. A booking with no premium has none in its record, and the paper confirmation says so: a premium is what the desk enters, never the model's value in its place. A premium is a finite amount with a three-letter currency and a date; a swaption's legs are booked as its holder's swap (the fixed leg paid by the holder of a payer swaption) on both sides, and a booking whose legs say otherwise, or whose right is not stated, is refused.

Until version 1.44 a swaption was signed under version 2 as the swap it delivers and nothing else: the record held no buyer or seller, no expiry and no premium, the trade's dates were the ticket's (the swap's as if it started at spot), and each leg's payer was read from the ticket's pay or receive choice, so a bought and a sold swaption were the same bytes and the sold one named the wrong payer. A confirmation signed under version 2 stays verifiable under version 2; its paper confirmation says what its record does not hold. The counterparty's own booking of an option on a rate, made from the countersigned record, copied the booker's side until this version, so both books held the same side of the option; it now holds the opposite one, and the two bookings derive the same version 3 record. \emph{Functions \code{signing\_version}, \code{\_canonical\_v3}; \code{api/routes/trade\_cards.\_mirror\_option\_terms}.}

\subsection{A package of trades confirmed as one}
A package (a strip, a collar, a forward with an option) is several trades between the same two parties, booked together under one trade ID with a leg ID for each. Each leg keeps its own unique transaction identifier and its own canonical record, as above. What the parties sign and the registry anchors, once, is the \emph{package record}: the schema name and version (\code{rijeka-package}, version 1), the two LEIs in sorted order and the keccak256 hashes of the legs' records, sorted. It commits to every term of every leg, and each side derives the same bytes from its own bookings; neither firm's reference for the package enters. A leg is proved by re-deriving its own record, finding its hash among the package's legs, and re-deriving the package hash, which is the hash the signatures cover and the registry holds. The legs are confirmed together or not at all. One leg of a package cannot yet be amended or terminated on its own, since that is a new package record signed by both parties. \emph{Functions \code{package_payload}, \code{package_hash}; route \code{/api/chain/confirm-package}.}

\subsection{Signatures}
Each party signs an EIP-712 typed message in the domain \code{Rijeka Trade Confirmation}, version 1, bound to the chain and the registry address:
\begin{center}
\code{TradeConfirmation(bytes32 tradeHash, address counterparty)}\\
\code{TradeAmendment(bytes32 prevHash, bytes32 newHash, address counterparty)}\\
\code{TradeTermination(bytes32 tradeHash, address counterparty)}
\end{center}
Each party's message names the \emph{other} party's address, so a signature cannot be replayed against a different counterparty, chain or registry. A firm's signing address is registered in Rijeka through a wallet challenge; once registered, Rijeka can verify that firm's signatures but can never produce them. On the hosted demo Rijeka holds keys for demo entities only, and any confirmation it signs for both sides is marked \code{bilateral: false}.

\subsection{The registry}
The registry contract has no owner and no administrative function. \code{confirm} verifies both signatures over the confirmation digest and records $(h, \text{partyA}, \text{partyB}, \text{timestamp})$ with status \emph{Confirmed}; \code{amend} verifies both signatures over the amendment digest, marks the previous hash \emph{Superseded} and records the new hash with the previous one as its parent; \code{terminate} verifies both signatures and sets \emph{Terminated}. Signatures with high $s$ values are rejected. Once a trade is anchored, Rijeka refuses off-chain edits to its terms: an amendment is itself a bilateral act.

\subsection{Verification without Rijeka}
A proof pack contains the canonical record, the hash, both signatures and the anchor. The public verifier, a single self-contained page, (i) re-serialises the record and recomputes the keccak256 hash, (ii) rebuilds both EIP-712 digests and recovers both signers, (iii) reads the registry on a public node and checks status and parties. No Rijeka server is contacted. The contract source is verified on Sourcify so the reader can audit what the registry does before trusting it. \textbf{What it proves:} that these exact terms were signed by these two keys and recorded at that block. \textbf{What it does not prove:} that a key belongs to a legal entity; that binding is a matter of the firms' own key management and registration.

\textbf{Causes of the convention differences.} Curve: the integer-year gap fill (\S\ref{sec:curve}). Swaps: market accrual adjustment and payment lag against Rijeka's default unadjusted accrual and the booked lag; under 0.1bp of notional. FX: the spot-date carry, now applied (the row keeps the old, valuation-date convention visible). Swaption delta: the ticket shows both the analytic forward delta $A\,\Phi(d)\cdot 1$bp with the annuity held and the curve DV01 (a $+1$bp parallel zero shift, repriced), which also moves the annuity; up to 14\% apart on a 5Y$\times$10Y. Risk and P\&L use the curve bump. Caps: the mid-period option time against QuantLib's compounded-rate model (\S\ref{sec:caps}). Exposure: the Hull--White implied vol a swaption is valued at on the paths against the market quote (\S\ref{sec:exposure}); the value today against QuantLib's Jamshidian price is within 0.35bp of notional, the paths reading the curve at one-day granularity. Ladder: a 1bp move in par quotes is a slightly smaller move in zero rates, so the ladder total sits about 2\% below the ticket's \code{IR01}; both are published and labelled.

\item \textbf{Validation.} The evidence is the QuantLib reference suite of \S\ref{sec:quantlib}, which reprices every product on every commit; what stands behind each model, and what does not, is in \code{docs/model\_inventory.md} and \code{docs/VALIDATION.md}. \item \textbf{Replications.} The Excel workbooks in the Methodology tab are regenerated on request from the user's own snapshot. Every book has the same shape: a cover with the trade and the checks' verdict; the ticket as the trader saw it, rebuilt in cells, every number a link into the sheet that derives it; the derivation, line by line, each quantity with its live value and its cell; the inputs, the curve and the product's own sheets; a scenario chart for the options; and a Checks sheet with every formula against the engine's value, a tolerance and a PASS or FAIL that the cover sums up. Plain spreadsheet features only, so the file opens clean in Excel, Numbers and Google Sheets.

The XVA book (\S\ref{sec:exposure}, \code{methodology/xva_workbook.py}) goes further: it carries the engine's own random draws, rebuilds every Hull--White path, the bond reconstitution, the margin-period bridge, the collateral and the waterfall in cells, shows the swaption calibration with the model's closed-form vol per basket point and an RMSE grid around the fit, and has a LIVE mode that re-rolls the simulation in the spreadsheet with a convergence sheet for the Monte Carlo error.

\item \textbf{Open items, stated plainly.} Hull--White is single-curve and calibrated to one tenor column (\S7--8). SABR is $\beta=0$ only. The hosted demo prices on a synthetic rates market (\S\ref{sec:mr}), synthetic volatility surfaces for rates and currency pairs, and public spots, none of it live, and says so on its screens. The registry contract has had no external audit. Initial margin is the desk's amount or the engine's estimate at current volatility; no licensed margin methodology is computed or claimed.
\item \textbf{Change control.} Every change to a pricing module is a commit in a public repository with its tests; this document is versioned with the code.
